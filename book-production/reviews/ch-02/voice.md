## Sensory anchors (≥3 numerically — PASS; distribution — RISK)

Total anchors across the chapter comfortably clear 3: granola bar and coffee (line 21), courthouse smell — "floor wax and stale coffee and somebody's nervous cologne" (line 67), the broken elevator and the diet-soda vending machine (line 35), trench mud and cold (line 39), the library carrel and the cold coffee beside the book (line 61).

But the prompt's diagnostic question is exactly right: **the middle doesn't carry any of it.** Lines 3–31 — the entire tournament/mechanism explanation (Axelrod's setup, the fourteen entrants, Rapoport's four lines, the second tournament, the ecological run, the four-properties breakdown) — is close to 1,000 words with no room, weather, body, sound, or meal anywhere in it. Every anchor in the chapter is parked in the Marisol frame or the trench case; the abstract argumentative core is as bodiless as the machine prose VOICE §1.5 warns about. It passes the letter of the metric only because the frame and the second case are carrying the whole chapter's sensory weight. Recommend: one small physical anchor inside the tournament section itself (Axelrod at a terminal, the physical form the "published report" took, anything) rather than relying entirely on Marisol's granola bar to retroactively ground it.

## Idiolect conformance (PASS, with one flagged tic)

Checked against all entries in `voice/IDIOLECT.md`:
- **Sentence shapes**: both present and clean. "It won." after the tournament build is shape #1. "Predictability, not decency, was what the arrangement needed..." followed by "On purpose, at real cost..." is shape #2 (short flat declarative, then a longer sentence that complicates it).
- **Image family**: present, correctly rationed. "Call it plumbing, if that helps you feel less silly about the granola bar" (line 67) is a light, single water-family touch; "one built out of shells and one built out of docket numbers" (line 59) is the engineering/machine half. No third family intrudes.
- **Joke shape**: present — "I was running FRIEDMAN on a person I'd known since I was nineteen and calling it integrity" (line 63) is understatement at the writer's own expense, correctly self-directed rather than aimed at anyone in the cases.
- **Named weakness (impatience first, verification second)**: enacted, not just claimed — the friend anecdote is structurally that exact weakness (judged fast, corrected six years later).
- **Refusals**: intact. Zero exclamation marks in prose (the only "!" hits are `<!--` comment delimiters). No "obviously." Chapter doesn't open on a quotation.
- **Two overused words**: weak. "Unreasonable" doesn't appear anywhere in the chapter. "Climbing" appears once (line 39) but literally — soldiers climbing out of a trench — not in its idiomatic escalation sense. Not a failure on its own (≥3 of 6 markers is the bar, and five others are solid), but worth noting the pair is going quiet.

**Flag — the rationed negation-and-correct construction is over budget.** IDIOLECT.md calls "X aren't being [vice]; [structural reason]" the book's central gesture *and* its most dangerous tic, and rations it to one cluster per chapter (up to three uses if it's genuinely doing the argument in a single passage), zero stray instances elsewhere. Ch-02 has three instances, and they are **not** clustered:
1. "He wasn't wrong to model his opponent... He was unlucky in a specific way" (line 31, re: Downing)
2. "You're not more virtuous than a stranger passing through. You just can't get away from each other." (line 35, re: Marisol)
3. "You didn't do anything shameful with that coffee, Marisol — you got there without needing six years, a funeral, or forty pages of proof" (line 67)

These are spread across a third of the chapter rather than doing the work together in one passage the way ch-01's "the programs aren't being greedy... the applicants aren't being neurotic... the deans aren't lying" cluster does. Individually each instance reads fine; as a set, the construction becomes visible as a tic exactly the way the idiolect sheet warns it will. This is the clearest, most mechanically checkable finding in this review and the one I'd send back for a look before v3 — likely fixed by cutting or reshaping one of the three (the Downing instance at line 31 is the least essential to the argument and the easiest cut).

## Same-writer verdict: PASS — strong

Ch-02 reads as the same author as all three frozen comparison units. Matches: the letter-to-a-named-skeptical-friend register (Nate → Priya → Marisol, all real, all addressed directly, all quoted saying the obvious objection back to the author — "This is just greed" in ch-01, "This is bureaucracy applied to conscience" in ch-10, "is this just self-interest with a longer memory" in ch-02); the citation discipline (exact page numbers, named researchers, a logged chain-of-sourcing caveat — "I'm getting it secondhand," line 37 — that mirrors ch-10's Milgram exclusion and ch-01's careful sourcing of Sinclair/Alverson/Marchak); the forward-tease chapter endings (ch-01 ends promising "the one structure... that doesn't need anybody... to be good," which ch-02 opens by fulfilling; ch-02 ends teasing Valencia); the confessional coffee-gone-cold signature used exactly once, correctly, at the genuine costly-admission beat (line 61) and nowhere else. No register break, no encyclopedia drift, no symmetry-completeness tell.

## Template-risk: beat order across the four units — MODERATE, watch

The "named interlocutor voices the chapter's obvious objection in their own quoted words, late in the chapter, and the author engages it seriously rather than dismissing it" beat now appears in the same rough structural slot (after the main case work, before the close) in ch-01 (Nate: "This is just greed"), ch-10 (Priya: "This is bureaucracy applied to conscience"), and ch-02 (Marisol, implicitly, on self-interest). Three-for-three in a row is enough repetition that a fourth or fifth chapter running the identical beat in the identical slot would start reading as formula rather than voice. Not a fix-now issue — flagging for the Continuity/Voice Curator pass at the next chapter so the move gets varied (skipped, moved earlier, or answered by someone other than the named interlocutor) rather than locked in as a template.
