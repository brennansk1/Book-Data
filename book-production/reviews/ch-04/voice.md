## 3. Deliberate obsession — PASS, but flag the phrasing

The Maghribi traders / genizah material (¶16–19, ~660 words) is disproportionate to what a third costly-signaling case strictly needs, and gets real texture (Schechter, Goitein, the Edwards/Ogilvie dispute) that the duel and Boehm cases don't. The obsession is real and well-executed as *content*.

The announcement is the problem: "This is where I'll admit to spending more time than the chapter strictly needs, because the story underneath the argument is one of my favorites in the whole book" (¶16). This doesn't just gesture at enthusiasm (compare ch-03's "genuinely strange, and my favorite part of the whole case," which passed clean) — it names the §1.3 mechanic itself ("more time than the chapter strictly needs"), which is narrating compliance with a VOICE checklist item while performing it, the exact second-order tell DL-4(a) exists to catch. Recommend cutting "than the chapter strictly needs" and keeping "one of my favorites in the whole book" — the obsession still reads, without the meta-commentary.

## 4. Deliberate omission — FAIL, two named omissions where the budget is exactly one

This is the finding the brief asked me to verify, and it's a real problem, though not the one the brief anticipated.

**Instance A** (¶24, the Mensur passage): "I found one claim about how far a scar like that could make a career climb, a specific figure for how many doors it opened decades later. I found it in exactly one place, and nowhere else that would corroborate it. So I'm leaving the number out and keeping only the shape of the claim." Read on its own, this does **not** read as a tease — it reads as a verification refusal, which is the right instinct and matches PRODUCTION_BIBLE §7.1 ("never invent a citation, quotation, statistic, or date"). It names what was withheld and why (uncorroborated, not scope), which is a materially different move from VOICE §1.3's model ("there's a whole literature on X, I'm not getting into it").

**Instance B** (¶23): "Both of my main cases here worked partly by leaving people out... The honor world Wellington belonged to was a closed shop by design... That's a thread for a later chapter, and it should worry you a little that I can already tell you where it's going." This is the clean, canonical §1.3 move — a real sub-topic, acknowledged, deferred for scope reasons — and it's doing real cross-chapter work (setting up the exclusivity critique DL entries elsewhere anticipate).

Both are legitimate individually. Together they exceed detection-log DL-4(c)'s explicit rule ("Named omissions: exactly ONE per chapter") and VOICE §10's target of 1. Recommend keeping B (it's load-bearing for later chapters) and rewriting A as a plain sourcing caveat without the omission-shaped frame — e.g., state the unverifiable claim's existence without "I'm leaving out / keeping only the shape of" phrasing, since that phrasing is what makes it read as a second omission rather than a footnote.

## 9. Idiolect conformance — mixed; three concrete findings

**Deflating shape** — present and well-placed twice: "No one had been hurt." after the duel buildup, and the two-line close ("I don't know yet." / "I'm not sure anyone does.") after the chapter's biggest concession. Matches the sheet's example ("Seventy.") cleanly.

**Short-then-complicating sentence shape** — present: "Then it died. Not slowly." followed by the longer sentence that undercuts the "not slowly" ("Wellington fires his pistol in 1829; by the 1850s the whole apparatus is essentially finished..."). Also "No one had been hurt. / No one on the field that morning had seriously expected anyone to be." across the paragraph break.

**Water/pressure image family — absent, and this is a real judgment call, not an obvious miss.** Zero occurrences of water, leak, plug, wall-as-metaphor, flow, drain, or pressure anywhere in the chapter. Checked against precedent: ch-02 (Axelrod/trenches) also has none, so total absence isn't unprecedented. The idiolect sheet's own language — "reach for water first when describing how **traps** behave" — is arguably why: ch-04 isn't describing a trap (a structure where individually sane choices produce collective ruin, the water-finds-the-gap shape). It's describing the opposite — a costly-signaling equilibrium that mostly *works*, then a closing concession that it might be "only a norm." Forcing the water image onto a chapter about honor and trade risks the DL-4(a) problem in reverse (bending the content to hit a checklist item), and the chapter reads clean without it. Flag for the Showrunner rather than the Line Editor: confirm the image family is meant to recur only in "trap" chapters, or whether it's supposed to appear book-wide regardless of subject.

**"Unreasonable" and "climbing"** — both present, once each, and both read natural rather than inserted. "Unreasonable" (¶21, Boehm): "A punishment like that would look wildly unreasonable coming from any one man alone, and somehow doesn't, coming from all of them together" — earns its place in the argument. "Climbing" appears as "career climb" (¶24, Mensur) rather than the gerund form used elsewhere in the book (prologue's "still climbing," ch-01's "climbing the same ladder") — same word family, different inflection; minor, not worth a fix.

**Refusals** — all three honored: zero exclamation marks (the only "!" matches in the file are HTML comment syntax, not punctuation), the chapter opens on a scene rather than a quotation, "obviously" does not appear.

**Named weakness (impatience first, verification second)** — present twice, and both instances are strong: "I told myself for a while that you'd gotten lucky with the line... without having read a page of what I'd later go read because of it" (¶17), and inside the ANCHOR, "I'd built the whole case fast, the way I write when I'm sure of where I'm headed, and it took a colder pass to find the seam I hadn't put there on purpose" (¶25). Good continuity with the established trait — but see §10 below on how this staging avoids one retired pattern while running into a different one.

**Negation-and-correct construction ("X isn't being [vice]; structural reason")** — absent entirely (confirmed by direct search). Given IDIOLECT.md calls this "the book's central rhetorical gesture," total absence is worth a note, though not a defect: this chapter's argument shape (individually-rational signaling that *works*, not individually-rational choices that produce collective ruin) doesn't have an obvious "nobody here is being [vice]" moment to hang the construction on. Reads as a deliberate structural difference, not an oversight.

**Coffee ration** — zero uses. Ceiling, not floor; absence isn't a defect.

## 12. Template risk in beat order — the strongest finding in this review

Five units in, and this chapter, the skeleton is now unmistakable: **cold dated scene → mechanism explained via 2–3 cases → one case gets a named obsession digression → named-friend's objection → ANCHOR concession that partially undercuts the chapter's own case → two-line calibrated-uncertainty close ("I'm confident about X. I'm [less/far less] sure about Y.")**

Every one of the six units now on the table (prologue, ch-01, ch-02, ch-03, ch-10, ch-04) ends on some form of the confidence/uncertainty formula:
- Prologue: "I'm confident about that part. I'm much less sure what anyone is supposed to do about it."
- Ch-02: "I'm confident about the first part... I'm far less sure about the second."
- Ch-03: "I'm confident in that much. I'm much less confident about how far..."
- Ch-10: "I'm confident about the first two gates. I'm a lot less sure where the trigger line should sit..."
- Ch-04 (¶28): "I'm confident about everything I've told you in the cases themselves... I'm far less confident that any of it answers you."

Six for six. This has moved past "recurring idiolect trait" into mechanical formula — it is very likely what a detection panel or a careful second read would flag first, because real essayists don't resolve every single piece with the identical hedge shape. This is a bigger risk than any individual metric miss above, and it compounds the DL-1 finding (§10): the chapter is now repeating two structural templates simultaneously (the announced-confession shape near the obsession/omission beat, and the calibrated-uncertainty close), which is exactly the kind of doubled repetition the detection log exists to catch before Part One's Gate 6 panel rather than after.

Recommend: before this chapter proceeds past Gate 3, vary the close. Options in the spirit of the book's existing register: end on the concession itself without restating it as a confidence/uncertainty pair; end on a question (as the prologue's *interior* close does, "Who do you forgive?", before its own confidence line); or let Dana get the last word instead of the writer. This is a Showrunner-level call, not a mechanical fix — flagging for the decisions log rather than resolving it here.
