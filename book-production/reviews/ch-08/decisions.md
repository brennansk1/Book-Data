# Ch-8 Decisions

**Gate 1 (2026-08-06, orchestrator as Showrunner proxy — see reviews/DEVIATIONS.md):**

- **Opening case: Frances Kelsey and thalidomide (1960–62), run as the INVERSION** the researcher proposed. She held a standing evidentiary requirement against sustained commercial pressure to make a case-by-case exception, and was vindicated by a catastrophe that never reached American nurseries. This opens better than a calculation-gone-wrong story because it pre-empts the act-consequentialist's easiest reply ("then calculate better") before he can make it: Kelsey's critics at the time WERE the better calculators, arguing the specific case on its specific merits.
- **Counterpoint case: the Casey/McFadden clemency decision (Pennsylvania, 1994)** — an individualized judgment that was defensible on the file in front of the decision-makers and catastrophic in outcome. Handle as decision structure, NOT as electoral spectacle: the 1994 race and the Willie-Horton optics are explicitly out of scope, because the chapter's claim is about how case-by-case judgment fails, not about how voters punish it.
- **EXCLUDED: Anna Pou / Memorial Medical Center.** Well documented and genuinely sympathetic, but the underlying facts of what was administered and why remain contested, and a chapter arguing for evidentiary humility cannot afford to narrate a disputed case as if settled. Reserved for Volume II if ever.
- **EXCLUDED: Makayla Sault** — a recently deceased child, decided on treaty-rights grounds rather than ordinary discretion. Wrong case, wrong cost.
