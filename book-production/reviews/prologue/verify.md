## PASS-WITH-NOTE (3)

11. **~$1,499 ERAS fee for 70 applications.** PASS-WITH-NOTE. The $1,499 figure is a verbatim
    Cureus quote ("A student applying to 70 programs — the current mean for U.S. students —
    pays $1,499 in ERAS fees alone") but it is **2020-vintage** ERAS pricing, and the draft
    places it in **the fee schedule "in effect that year"** — i.e., September 2022, Kudrna's
    cycle. I reconstructed the actual 2022–2023 ERAS tier schedule ($99 for apps 1–10, $19
    each for 11–20, $23 each for 21–30, $27 each for 31+) and it prices 70 applications at
    **$1,599**, about $100 (~7%) more than $1,499. Fix: either attribute $1,499 explicitly to
    "the 2020 mean cost" rather than implying it's the exact cost Kudrna paid in Sept 2022, or
    update the figure to ~$1,600 for the scene it's actually dramatizing. Source for tier
    math: https://blog.matcharesident.com/fees-for-the-2023-24-us-medical-residency-application-cycle/

12. **Signaling reform / "dermatology ~70→~40 in two years."** PASS-WITH-NOTE, and the
    draft's own hedge ("by one tally") is doing real, necessary work here — do not remove it.
    Direction is well-supported (multiple sources confirm a 2024–2025 cycle-wide decline in
    applications per applicant, credited to Program Signaling, with individual specialties
    seeing 30–45% drops). But the specific "~70 to ~40 in two years" pairing for dermatology
    could not be pinned to one authoritative AAMC table in this session — a different
    secondary-source table (medschoolcoach.com, AAMC-derived) shows dermatology at 72 (2019),
    57 (2024), 34 (2026) — a different shape and timeline than "70→40 in two years." The
    underlying direction and rough magnitude are real; the precise number-pair is genuinely
    contested across sources. Keep "by one tally" or make it explicitly softer (e.g., "by some
    tallies, dermatology applications roughly halved within a few cycles").

13. **Program-director survey ranking research second.** PASS-WITH-NOTE. STAT (Shrestha)
    states research is "the second most important aspect" of applications and cites this to
    the **NRMP 2022 Program Director Survey Report**. I independently pulled that survey and
    its own "most frequently considered" ranking for interview-grant decisions lists Step 1
    pass/fail (86%), the MSPE/dean's letter (85%), specialty LORs (84%), Step 2 score (83%),
    and the personal statement (81%) — a different framing/metric than what Shrestha's "second
    most important" gloss implies. This isn't a contradiction of the draft's claim (which
    accurately reports what the STAT piece says), but the STAT piece's own characterization of
    the NRMP data is looser than the primary document — worth knowing if a sharper citation is
    ever needed. No text change required unless the passage is tightened to cite NRMP directly
    rather than via STAT. — https://www.nrmp.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PD-Survey-Report-2022_FINALrev.pdf

## FAIL (2)

14. **"Nobody in Washington unfroze the other side of the equation."** FAIL as stated —
    needs a precise fix. The Balanced Budget Act of 1997 mechanism and motivation are
    accurate: it froze Medicare-funded direct/indirect GME resident caps at 1996 levels,
    on hospital-specific bases (recomputed via 3-year rolling average thereafter), acting on
    the 1994 COGME (Council on Graduate Medical Education) recommendation, itself built on a
    predicted physician surplus (~70,000) that never materialized. **However**, Congress
    *did* partially unfreeze the cap: the **Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021** (Section
    126) added **1,000 new Medicare-funded GME slots**, phased in at up to 200/year starting
    FY2023, distributed to qualifying hospitals (rural, over-cap, new-med-school-state, or
    HPSA-serving). As of September 2025, CMS had allocated 600 of the 1,000. This directly
    contradicts the literal claim that "nobody" unfroze the other side. Recommended fix:
    acknowledge the 2021 thaw while noting its scale — 1,000 slots (phased over five years)
    against a base of roughly 140,000 funded positions and against tens of thousands of new
    medical-school graduates since 1997 is a rounding error, not a rebalancing. Something
    like: *"Congress added a token 1,000 slots in 2021 — phased in over five years, against a
    base of well over a hundred thousand — but never lifted the cap itself."* Sources:
    https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/fund-reports/1997/dec/balanced-budget-act-1997-implications-graduate-medical-education
    ;
    https://www.ama-assn.org/education/gme-funding/1000-new-gme-slots-are-coming-cms-must-not-hamper-their-use
    ; GAO, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107686

15. **"By some counts, the average unmatched applicant now lists more research activity than
    the average matched applicant did ten years ago."** FAIL — unverifiable, likely a
    mis-extraction. The evidence packet (`research/prologue/evidence.md`) attributes this
    claim to Shrestha's STAT piece, flagged there as REPORTED-BUT-SINGLE-SOURCE. I fetched the
    STAT article directly, twice, with a request to reproduce every sentence mentioning
    "unmatched," "matched," "a decade," or "10 years" — **the claim does not appear in the
    article at all.** A subsequent independent search found no other source making this
    specific comparison. Actual research-productivity data I could find (e.g., otolaryngology:
    matched applicants' average research items rose from 8.4 to 17.2 between 2016 and 2022,
    while 2022's unmatched applicants averaged 11.0 — roughly matched applicants' 2020 level)
    shows a real "creep" phenomenon but does not support this specific unmatched-vs.-matched-
    decade-ago framing. Recommend cutting the sentence, or replacing it with a claim that is
    actually traceable to a source — e.g., the otolaryngology matched-applicant research-item
    trend above, properly cited and specialty-scoped. Do not publish this sentence as currently
    sourced. — https://www.statnews.com/2024/04/29/research-arms-race-medical-students-residency-applications/
    (confirmed absent); no corroborating source found elsewhere.
