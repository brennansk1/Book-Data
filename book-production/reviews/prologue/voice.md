# Voice Curator Review — Prologue, draft-v2.md

Scored against `VOICE.md` §10 judgment metrics (the ones `tools/lint.py` and `tools/burstiness.py` cannot compute). Cross-referenced against `voice/seed/models/` (interim corpus: Haidt, Galef, Dawkins) and `voice/seed/anti/` (anti-1, anti-2, anti-3, drawn from the first-edition backup).

**Overall verdict: PASS, with one structural wobble that needs a Line Editor pass before Gate 3, and one unresolved production flag (the niece anecdote) that the Showrunner must resolve before this can clear Gate 4.**

The draft sits much closer to the `models/` register than the `anti/` register — it opens cold on a case exactly the way interim-1 (Haidt) and interim-2 (Galef) do, withholds its thesis until the case has landed, uses first-person singular throughout with no institutional "we," and contains a genuinely unflattering self-admission at its load-bearing point. But one paragraph (line 35) reproduces, almost exactly, the triple negation-and-correct rhythm that `anti/anti-3.md` names as a specific failure pattern. That is the top risk below.

## 3. Deliberate obsession — SOFT PASS, ambiguous (need 1)

The clearest candidate is the research-arms-race tangent (line 42): the piece has already made its point once the ERAS-signaling fix is shown to have partially failed, but it goes on for a full paragraph into neurosurgery research counts, the Shrestha citation, and "the bar didn't just rise, it rose out from under the people trying to clear it." That's more space than the argument strictly needs — one sentence ("the same pressure just resurfaced as a research arms race") would have made the logical point. The extra space reads as the author finding this particular second-order collapse interesting in its own right, which is what §1.3 wants.

The caveat: because the whole Prologue is built as a single sustained case (per the Sandel/Haidt case-first steal), it's harder than usual to distinguish "obsession" from "the case, which is supposed to be long." I'd score this a pass but flag it as the weakest of the eight metrics to call with confidence — the Showrunner should confirm this reads as authorial interest and not just as more case.

## Idiolect extraction — proposed seed for the book's idiolect sheet

No Showrunner idiolect sheet exists yet (VOICE §2). This draft is the first data point. Extracted from what it actually does, repeatedly and apparently on purpose — **5 of the 6 required categories have usable candidates; recommend locking in at minimum the three marked "confident."**

**1. Sentence shapes — confident.** Two moves recur:
- *Buildup paragraph, then a flat one- or two-word sentence as its own paragraph, functioning as a deflating or ironic beat.* "Every year the number goes up anyway." / "Seventy." / "Somewhere the total is still climbing." This is the draft's single strongest tic and reads as intentional, not accidental.
- *A closing rhetorical question, sometimes doubled.* "Who, exactly, do you get angry at? / Who do you forgive?" closes the whole piece this way; smaller versions occur mid-draft. Matches the Sandel/Appiah "leave the case open" move in the models corpus.

**2. Recurring image family — needs a Showrunner decision.** The draft's organic image family is **quantification and pressure**, not the engineering/bridge family VOICE §2 suggests as the default (bridge, fence, firmware). Recurring images: running totals ("the total in the corner," "the total is still climbing," "arithmetic"), and a secondary water/overflow motif ("drowning in files," "You plug one hole and the water finds a different wall"). Both threads are used more than once and both are doing real work. **Flag for the Showrunner:** either commit to quantification+pressure as the book's actual image family (in which case update VOICE §2 to match what the Prologue already does), or deliberately fold future chapters back toward the engineering family the framework already leans on. Don't let both run loose — VOICE §2 explicitly warns against mixing four metaphor families per chapter, and if the Prologue's water motif and a later chapter's bridge motif both recur without coordination, that reads as inconsistency, not richness.

**3. Joke shape — confident.** Dry understatement, consistently. "Nobody in this is doing anything unreasonable." / "That is one program's number, not a national one — but the clinic hours are real." / "I think that's real progress, for what it's worth." This is exactly the "safest" shape VOICE §2 recommends, and it's already the draft's only humor register — no other joke type appears, which is correct discipline for one book-wide shape.

**4. Two overused words — confident.** *"Unreasonable/reasonable"* recurs four times (lines 15, 19, 33, 46) and is thematically load-bearing — it's the word the whole Prologue is organized around refuting ("nobody is being unreasonable" is close to the thesis). *"Climb/climbing"* recurs three times (lines 11, 17, 38) as the running visual for the number of applications. Both are legitimate candidates for the two words VOICE §2 asks the book to let recur.

**5. Named rhetorical weakness — confident, and already executed correctly.** The Anchor states it directly: "It's a pattern I recognize in myself now, generally: impatience first, the slower work of actually checking a distant second. I'm not proud of the order." This is precisely the move VOICE §2 describes — a habit named as a habit, which is itself a costly signal. Recommend carrying "impatience before verification" forward explicitly as the book's named authorial weakness rather than re-deriving a new one per chapter.

**6. One thing the author refuses to do — tentative.** Zero exclamation marks and zero uses of "obviously" across 1,754 words, despite several moments of real feeling (the quotes from Kudrna and Harrison, the irritation admission). Worth naming as a refusal going forward, but one chapter isn't enough evidence to lock it in — confirm against the next 1–2 frozen chapters before writing it into the permanent sheet.

**Recommendation:** promote categories 1, 3, 4, and 5 to the idiolect sheet now; resolve category 2 as a Showrunner decision before Chapter 1 drafts; hold category 6 for confirmation.

## Where it wobbles toward the anti/ register — the top voice risk

**Line 35 reproduces the exact failure pattern `anti/anti-3.md` names.** Anti-3's diagnosis of the first-edition draft was: *"The 'not X, but Y' tic fires four times in this short span... STYLE_BIBLE §3 caps this frame at 2 per chapter (hard fail above 4) — here it is both the mid-paragraph rhythm and the closing move."* Line 35 of this draft does the same thing three times in a row, in the same paragraph:

> "The programs **aren't being** greedy; an empty seat in July is a real cost... The applicants **aren't being** neurotic; the student who applies to twenty-five programs like it's 2011 might genuinely not match anywhere... The deans **aren't lying** when they tell a room full of anxious twenty-six-year-olds not to do this; the advice is true, for any one student, taken alone."

And line 46 runs the same shape again with a different verb: *"'Everyone loses' **isn't right**... 'Nobody's fault' **isn't right either**; it makes it sound like an accident, and it **isn't**."* Combined, that's five near-identical negation-and-correct constructions across a 1,754-word draft — over the anti-corpus's own stated cap of 2 per chapter, and close to its hard-fail line of 4, counting line 35 alone.

This is the draft's one real wobble. It is doing genuine work — the triple in line 35 is the paragraph that carries the Prologue's actual thesis (everyone is locally rational, the system is still bad), and the rhythm is arguably earning its keep there in a way anti-3's version wasn't (anti-3's version was institutional "we" stacked on tricolon stacked on quote-decoration; this is first-person, concrete, and each clause has a different real-world referent). But it is structurally the same tic the anti-corpus was built to flag, run back-to-back rather than spaced through the chapter, and it should not survive a Line Editor pass unexamined. **Recommend: keep one instance (the strongest — probably the deans/advice clause, since it sets up the Anchor), cut or rephrase the other two in line 35, and rewrite one of the two in line 46 as a straight declarative instead of a negated one.**

Everything else in the draft — the case-first cold open, the first-person singular throughout, the absence of any institutional "we," the quotes used as evidence rather than decoration, the absence of lists or a summary section, the unresolved final question instead of a stated moral — reads as the models/ register, not the anti/ register. This is a genuinely strong draft. The wobble above and the placeholder anecdote are the two things standing between this and a clean Gate 3.
