## Boisjoly's July 1985 memo — FAIL (misattribution)

9. **Draft: "...warning that erosion in the solid rocket boosters' O-rings could kill someone, and asking that a team be assigned to the problem before the company found itself explaining the failure to a board of inquiry."** FAIL. The memo's actual closing language is: *"It is my honest and very real fear that if we do not take immediate action to dedicate a team to solve the problem with the field joint having the number one priority, then we stand in jeopardy of losing a flight along with all the launch pad facilities."* and separately, *"The result would be a catastrophe of the highest order — loss of human life."* Neither sentence mentions a "board of inquiry." That specific phrase — *"If anything happens to this launch, I wouldn't want to be the person that has to stand in front of a board of inquiry to explain why we launched"* — was said by a different person (Thiokol's Al McDonald) at a different moment (the January 27, 1986 teleconference the night before launch, six months after the memo), not written by Boisjoly in the July 31, 1985 memo. The draft's paraphrase conflates the two. **Fix:** either drop "before the company found itself explaining the failure to a board of inquiry" from the memo description (replace with the memo's actual stakes — loss of a flight/facilities, loss of human life), or move the "board of inquiry" image to where McDonald actually said it in the Jan. 27 call. Sources: memo text — https://www.onlineethics.org/cms/12703.aspx and https://lettersofnote.com/2009/10/27/the-result-would-be-a-catastrophe/ (full memo); McDonald's actual "board of inquiry" line — https://www.wvik.org/2021-03-07/remembering-allan-mcdonald-he-refused-to-approve-challenger-launch-exposed-cover-up

## Diane Vaughan / normalization of deviance

10. **Draft's characterization (para. 17): O-ring erosion "observed, discussed, and reclassified as acceptable risk across a long run of earlier flights," each surviving launch became "precedent for the next," nobody knew they were "breaking a rule," the rule itself had "drifted."** PASS. ## Loewenstein hot-cold empathy gap — FAIL (year/attribution of the *naming*)

11. **Draft: "the hot-cold empathy gap, which he named in a 1996 paper on visceral influences on behavior."** FAIL as worded. Author (Loewenstein) is correct, and the concept traces to his 1996 paper "Out of Control: Visceral Influences on Behavior" (*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 65(3), 272–292), which does discuss intrapersonal/interpersonal gaps in predicting visceral-state-driven behavior. But the specific term "hot-cold empathy gap" was not coined/named in that 1996 paper — it was formally introduced and named in Loewenstein, "Hot-Cold Empathy Gaps and Medical Decision Making," *Health Psychology* 24(4 Suppl.), S49–S56, **2005**. Multiple independent sources confirm the 1996 paper laid the conceptual foundation but the term itself dates to 2005. **Fix:** either change the citation to 2005 ("a 2005 paper on medical decision-making, building on his 1996 work on visceral influences"), or soften "named" to something like "a concept he's been developing since a 1996 paper on visceral influences on behavior, and formally named in 2005." As currently written, the sentence asserts the 1996 paper is where the term was coined, which is not accurate. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-cold_empathy_gap ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045419/ ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7701109_Hot-Cold_Empathy_Gap_and_Medical_Decision_Making

(Note: the draft correctly does NOT attribute this to Kahneman — Kahneman's System 1/System 2 distinction is invoked separately, earlier in the chapter, for a different point. That separation is correct and avoids the mix-up the Researcher flagged.)

## The 2036 prediction — PASS-WITH-NOTE (phrasing flag, as requested)

A 2036 adjudicator would need to pick a specific ranking/metric/publication-year snapshot to check this claim, and the draft doesn't specify one. **Fix:** name the metric and source snapshot the prediction should be checked against (e.g., "the top twenty U.S. health systems by patient revenue per Becker's Hospital Review's [year] ranking") so the bet is falsifiable in 2036 rather than adjudicator-dependent.
