# Cold Read — ch-10 draft-v1 ("Three Gates")

Method: read STYLE_BIBLE §1–4 and VOICE §1, then draft-v1.md once, straight through, no notes taken on a second pass.

Overall: this reads better than "framework document" — one consistent essayist voice, real costly admissions, a genuine bookend with Priya. The findings below are the real friction points, not padding.

## Findings (severity order)

**1. The chapter's own gate count doesn't add up on a single read.**
Gate 1 (trigger) and Gate 2 are labeled explicitly: "If it does clear, **the second gate** is a delay." Gate 3 never gets the same treatment — the review is introduced with "Then, no matter what you decide... there's a review," no "third gate" tag anywhere near it. Then: "Only after **those two gates** does the substance start to matter at all" — which retroactively excludes the review from the gate count altogether. So is the review Gate 3, and "substance" its content? Or is substance itself Gate 3, and the review something else again? I had to reread twice to guess, and I'm still not certain I guessed right. Given the chapter is titled *Three Gates*, this is the load-bearing structural label, and it's the one piece of scaffolding the prose doesn't actually give me.

**2. "The five conditions" — I could only ever find four.**
Para on substance ("Whether the harm was really caused by the rule... Whether you exhausted the alternatives... Whether you could say what you did out loud... Whether your own interest in the outcome has been scrubbed out...") gives four conditions. Later: "Naming which condition is load-bearing for your particular role is most of the actual work. **The five conditions** read the same on the page for everyone." I went back and recounted — causation, last resort, articulability, self-interest. Four. A skeptical reader will do the same arithmetic I just did and wonder what got cut.

**3. Two passages read as a list wearing prose's clothes.**
The four "Whether..." sentences (above) and, later, "A soldier has a chain of command... A civil servant usually has an inspector general's office... A physician like Priya has a hospital ethics board... A private citizen with no institution above them has almost nothing" — both are structurally enumerations, not connected reasoning; nothing in either passage explains why item two follows from item one, they just accumulate. No literal bullets, so it slides past a formatting check, but it reads exactly like the thing rule 5 is trying to prevent. This is the direct answer to "do the three gates arrive as connected reasoning or a hidden list" — the *gates* mostly do (trigger → delay → review is genuinely sequential, each one earns the next). The *substance conditions* and the *role taxonomy* don't.

**4. Attention drifted here:** "There's a concept from the Carnegie Mellon economist George Loewenstein: the hot-cold empathy gap. He named it in a 1996 paper on visceral influences on behavior, and again in a 2005 paper on medical decision-making specifically." Two dated citations back to back is the one moment the essay voice drops out and something closer to a lit review shows up. It's brief and the chapter recovers immediately, but it's the exact sentence where I stopped hearing a person and started hearing a footnote.

**5. Seam, per your framing.** The joint between the WorldCom narrative and the analytical machinery is audible right at "Which means the gates can't be one fixed threshold for everybody" — the Cooper story (lamplit, specific, human: "auditors working after hours by lamp light") stops cold and the prose snaps into the role taxonomy from finding 3. That's where disaster-narrative register hands off to procedural register hardest; everywhere else the handoffs are cushioned by a direct line ("Run Priya's code back through this... Here's a harder version of Priya's objection"), but this one isn't cushioned at all.

**6. Boisjoly is quoted twice** — the 1985 memo ("a catastrophe of the highest order — loss of human life") and later, separately, his testimony ("At that moment I felt totally helpless..."). Both land individually. Together they're two quotes from one source in one chapter.

**7. The 2036 prediction reads as a costly signal that's been de-fanged.** "By 2036, I'd bet most hospital systems will have quietly built **some version of** a sealed, non-routed reporting channel." A dated bet is supposed to be checkable and risk something. "Some version of" makes it true almost no matter what happens. It's the one place the chapter's vulnerability felt performed rather than real — everywhere else (the job, the friendship, "I'm still a little angry... at a panel whose members are almost certainly dead") the cost is genuine.

**8. A "not X, [it's] Y" tic recurs past comfortable range** and clusters late: "...which is not a standard, it's an absence of one" / "Not through some rogue official ignoring the rules. Through the review process itself..." / "Not correction. Attrition..." / "That's what makes it worse, not better" / "Not a system for people who might be wrong... A system for people who...". Each instance is fine alone; by the Tuskegee section it starts to sound like a habit rather than a choice.

## Direct answers to your six questions

- **The argument, one sentence:** I could not do this cleanly after one read. My best attempt needed a "but": *You need a dumb bright-line trigger and a mandatory cooling-off delay before your reasons are even allowed to matter, and a permanent no-fault review after — but the whole thing assumes the institution around you isn't captured, and when it is, the gates have to protect the person who complies too much, not just the one who overrides too fast.* That's two arguments stitched at the Buxtun turn, not one. See finding 1 — I think the missing "third gate" label is a lot of why the compression doesn't hold.

- **Trust:** one voice throughout, and it holds — Priya opens and closes the chapter, the confession about the job and the friendship is genuinely unflattering, and I never caught a moment that felt safe-costume-vulnerability except finding 7.

- **Buxtun concession:** feels real, not staged. It doesn't just note a caveat — it goes back and reopens the machinery ("They need a second set of eyes turned on false negatives... Otherwise the chapter is only teaching half the skill"), and the proposed fix for it is honestly thin, which the chapter admits rather than papering over. That thinness is a feature of the honesty, not a separate problem.

- **Factual side-eye:** only finding 2 (the count mismatch) — everything else checked against memory (Challenger's 53°F floor, the GM ignition switch figures, Tuskegee's timeline, WorldCom's $3.8B and the 2002 Time whistleblower cover) held up.
