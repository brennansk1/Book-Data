## 9. Idiolect conformance — mixed, with one clear finding

**Deflating shape** — present and well-placed: "Pennies." after the fine-books setup is a direct match to the sheet's example ("Seventy."). The rhythm also shows up at the paragraph-break level rather than within one paragraph — "None of it saved them." (short, flat) followed immediately by "Good rules, honestly arrived at and faithfully kept for a generation, turned out to be exactly as durable as the willingness of everyone outside the village to leave them alone." (longer, complicates/undercuts it) — a variant deployment of sentence-shape #2 across a paragraph break rather than within one. Effective, and worth noting as a legitimate variation rather than a miss.

**Water/pressure family — double duty, not over-saturated.** The chapter uses "water" 18 times, but the overwhelming majority is literal (the Tribunal, the canals, the huerta) rather than metaphorical — it has to be, since water is the chapter's actual subject. The idiolect's *signature move* (water finding the gap you didn't seal) is deployed exactly twice, and both deployments earn it:
1. ¶33, on the boundary principle: "Water doesn't ask permission before it finds the lowest opening; a wall only means something once everyone agrees where it starts and stops." — literal water fact and structural argument in the same sentence.
2. ¶65, transplanted onto Mawelle — a fishing-net case, not a water-rights case: "water finding a hole in a wall nobody had reinforced, because nobody building the wall had planned for a wall-breaker with a friend in Parliament." Applying the image to a non-water case is what proves it's functioning as the book's recurring idiolect image rather than as incidental subject-matter vocabulary — it survives being moved off-topic.

Verdict: not over-saturated. Two purposeful deployments amid a large field of necessary literal usage is restrained, not indulgent.

**Understatement/joke shape** — present: "Nobody but me ever saw it, and I'd like to keep it that way, except that I just told you." (ANCHOR) is a clean, self-deprecating instance.

**Two overused words** — one present, one missing. "Climbing" appears once (¶61, Mawelle net count), consistent with its rate in the frozen chapters (1 use each in prologue/ch-01/ch-02, 0 in ch-10). **"Unreasonable" does not appear in this chapter at all** — a gap. It appears in all four frozen units (3 in prologue, 1 each in ch-01/ch-02/ch-10). This is the one clean idiolect miss in the draft: a book-level signature word absent from a chapter with no shortage of individually-reasonable-actor material to hang it on (the Mawelle villagers, the farmer under pressure, Crosbie-adjacent DFO-style institutional actors are exactly the terrain "unreasonable" usually does its work in elsewhere in the book).

**Refusals** — all three honored: zero exclamation marks, chapter does not open on a quotation, "obviously" does not appear.

**Negation budget — the chapter's real finding.** IDIOLECT.md budgets the "X isn't being [vice]; [structural reason]" move at one cluster per chapter (up to three instances if they're in a single passage where it *is* the argument), zero stray instances elsewhere. This draft has three instances, and they are not clustered — they're scattered across the chapter at three unrelated beats:
- ¶37 (stag hunt): "Nobody has to be greedy for a stag hunt to fail — they just have to doubt, for one bad morning, that the other person will show up."
- ¶47 (quasi-voluntary compliance): "isn't a criminal. He's a person under pressure, and hitting him with a punishing fine..."
- ¶65 (Mawelle): "Nobody in that village was foolish or lazy or bad at cooperation. A politician with a favor to sell walked in from outside..."

Each instance is well-written on its own. The problem is dispersion, not quality: three separate stray uses of the book's most-flagged tic (IDIOLECT.md calls it "the book's central rhetorical gesture and its most dangerous tic") reads, at a chapter level, like the construction running on autopilot rather than being spent once, deliberately, where the argument needs it most. Recommend cutting to one: keep ¶47 (it's load-bearing — it's the quasi-voluntary-compliance mechanism itself) and rewrite ¶37 and ¶65 without the negate-then-correct frame.

**Coffee ration** — not used. Zero occurrences of "coffee" anywhere in the chapter. This is a ceiling (max once), not a floor, so its absence isn't a defect, just a note that the chapter didn't reach for it.

## 10. Same-writer verdict vs. all four frozen units

**Yes, with one flagged seam.** The prose passes as the same hand: the deflating one-word paragraph, the short-then-complicating rhythm, the understatement joke, the three refusals, and the double-duty water metaphor all check out against the seed corpus established by the frozen chapters. The high-band diction (regadiu/secano/syndic/hereters) matches the register the frozen units set with their own technical borrowings (Elster/precommitment in ch-10, Schelling's micromotives/macrobehavior in ch-01).

The seam: the missing "unreasonable" and the dispersed (rather than clustered) negation move are both small, but they're the kind of small that a detection panel or a close second read would catch, because they're exactly the two idiolect markers IDIOLECT.md names as highest-risk ("its most dangerous tic," and one of only two words the sheet requires to recur). Fix the negation dispersion; consider one deliberate "unreasonable" placement (the Mawelle villagers or the farmer-under-pressure passage are both natural homes for it).

## 11. Template risk in beat order

Structural shape matches the established template closely: cold case-open → interlocutor revealed a few paragraphs in → mechanism explained → author's obsession digression → failure/counter-case → concession → transition line teeing up the next chapter. This is by design (Pass A/B/C protocol) and isn't itself a problem.

Two specific repeats worth flagging for the Part One Gate 6 panel:
1. **The ANCHOR's confession shape repeats ch-01's.** Ch-01's anchor: "It's a pattern I recognize in myself now, generally: impatience first, the slower work of actually checking a distant second." Ch-03's anchor: "That's its own small case study in the thing I like least about myself: reach the verdict fast, do the slower checking later, if ever." Different anecdote (a niece's arithmetic vs. dismissing Ostrom), same named weakness, restated in near-identical phrasing. This is *correct* per IDIOLECT.md's "named weakness, used anyway" requirement — it's supposed to recur — but the phrasing is close enough to risk reading as a template rather than a recurring trait when the two chapters are read back to back. It does not trip DL-1 (the admission enters cold/after-the-fact here, not pre-announced), so it's not a hard violation, just a proximity risk.
2. **Interlocutor-placement (DL-3) is correctly varied.** Grace's objection ("Communes are nice. This doesn't scale.") lands at ¶19, roughly a quarter into the chapter — the "objection arrives first, chapter answers it" option DL-3 flagged ch-03 as the candidate for. This is the one chapter of the four that doesn't put the friend's pushback in the back third. Good — the template-risk fix from the detection log was actually implemented.
