## Cabot 1497

34. **"John Cabot wrote home about it in 1497, how thick the fish were, sailors claiming you could slow a ship down just by sailing it through them."** PASS-WITH-NOTE (attribution imprecision). The "fish so thick" account does not come from a surviving letter written by Cabot himself — there is no known Cabot letter. The earliest report is a December 18, 1497 letter from Raimondo di Soncino, the Milanese envoy in London, to the Duke of Milan, relaying what Cabot and his crew reported on their return ("the sea there is swarming with fish which can be taken not only with the net but in baskets let down with a stone"). The draft's "sailors claiming you could slow a ship down" is correctly hedged as lore, but "Cabot wrote home about it" misattributes authorship/medium. **Fix:** change to something like "An Italian envoy in London wrote home about it that December, passing along what Cabot's crew had told him" or "Word of it reached Europe within months — how thick the fish were, sailors claiming..." — i.e., attribute the report to Soncino (or simply "reports reached Europe"), not to Cabot personally writing home. [Elfinspell primary-source text of Soncino's letter](https://elfinspell.com/PrimarySourceCabot.html)
