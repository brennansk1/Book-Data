# Cold Read — ch-01/draft-v1.md

Single read-through, no prior context beyond STYLE_BIBLE.md §1–4 and VOICE.md §1. Severity-ordered.

---

## 1. The argument, and whether the honest causality helps or hurts it (highest severity)

**One-sentence restatement after one read:** Individually reasonable people, each following their own local incentive with no villain among them, can still produce a collective catastrophe — and this "shape" recurs (cod fishery, Amazon HQ2 bidding) whether or not anyone is watching.

**Does the nuance clarify or muddy?** Mostly muddies, and the chapter half-notices this itself without fixing it.

The commons-tragedy structure requires *many independent actors*, each imposing a small externalized cost, summing to ruin. The chapter's own research splits the cod story into two causally different things:

- A genuine multi-agent commons dynamic: "the dominant driver after 1977... was Canada's own industrial fleet, each company running boats it was, from where it sat, entirely reasonable to run" (para. 35). This *is* the shape the chapter is teaching.
- A single-institution failure: DFO/CAFSAC had a broken measuring model, was told it was broken by its own commissioned audit, and kept fishing anyway for years. This is not a multi-agent coordination trap — it's one body ignoring its own evidence. It's a different animal (closer to bureaucratic normalization-of-deviance than to Hardin's herdsmen), and the chapter spends more words on it than on the fleet-expansion dynamic.

The chapter surfaces the exact objection that undercuts its own frame — Marchak's argument "that it isn't really a Hardin-style commons at all, but a tragedy of state mismanagement instead, since the fishermen never held any of the management rights themselves" (para. 55) — and then declines to resolve it: "I'm not going to adjudicate the whole academic fight... Pick the label you like." That's the load-bearing sentence of the whole risk you flagged, and it's a shrug where the chapter needs a distinction. A skeptical reader who's been paying attention has just been handed the tools to conclude "so it wasn't really a commons tragedy, it was one government agency screwing up" — which breaks the parallel to Amazon HQ2 (a genuinely multi-agent race, 238 cities) that the chapter needs for its second structure to land as *the same shape, different mechanism* rather than *unrelated stories, retrofitted label*.

The fix isn't to cut the honest material — cutting it would be worse and dishonest. It's to explicitly separate the two causal threads: name the fleet-expansion as the actual commons instantiation, and name the DFO failure as a *different, adjacent* pathology (worth its own future chapter?) rather than folding both under one contested label and moving on.

## 2. Pacing — the commons section overstays (high severity)

Roughly 65 of 96 lines (~70% of the chapter) are spent on the cod collapse before Amazon even appears at line 69. The Hardin digression is self-flagged as deliberate excess ("I'm going to spend a little longer on Hardin than the argument strictly needs," para. 46) — that's the VOICE.md-mandated "obsession," fine on its own terms. But combined with the CAFSAC data, the Finlayson research, and the Marchak aside, the cumulative effect is that Amazon reads thin by comparison: one paragraph of facts (71), one of collapse (73), one of context (75), then straight into objection-handling. The second structure, which is supposed to be the cleaner, less-hedged half of the lesson, arrives rushed and doesn't get room to breathe the way cod did. A reader finishing the chapter is more likely to be able to re-explain the cod case in detail than the HQ2 case.

## 3. Seams (moderate severity)

The joint between case one and case two is announced rather than felt: "Okay. Deep breath. Second case, because one example is an anecdote and you'll ask me for a second one anyway" (para. 69). This is honest about being a structural device — which is exactly the problem. It tells the reader "here is example two, provided for methodological completeness," which is closer to the framework move the register is supposed to avoid (rule 1: "book prose has structure and doesn't mention it"). The line before it — "It's a question for later in this book, not this letter" (para. 67) — also briefly steps outside the letter fiction to talk about the book as an object, which is a small crack in an otherwise well-sustained frame.

The Hardin/Ostrom digression (paras. 45–53) is its own seam: it's a clean, contained history-of-ideas essay dropped into the middle of the fishery narrative, sign-posted with "There's a name for what almost happened here" going in and closed with a return to "Cod, though, was managed, at least on paper" coming out. The seams are stitched competently (the Alverson quote and the Sinclair quote both do double duty, carrying both fact and voice), but the essay-within-a-letter quality is detectable — it's the one stretch where the "letter to Nate" device goes quiet for nine paragraphs.

## 4. Register failures (low-moderate severity)

Two sentences read as authorial/structural intrusion rather than the letter voice:

- **"There's a name for what almost happened here, and it's worth pausing on, because it's the most famous name in this whole territory and also the most misused."** (para. 45) — "it's worth pausing on" is a close cousin of the banned "it's worth noting that." It signals *I am about to digress for your benefit* instead of just digressing.
- **"Two structures explained properly beat five explained badly."** (para. 89) — this is a pedagogical design note, not something a person says mid-letter. It states the chapter's own curricular logic (why two structures, not five) out loud, which is the exact move rule 1 prohibits: structure that announces itself.

Nothing from the banned-vocabulary list (delve, tapestry, leverage, nuanced, robust, etc.) appears anywhere in the chapter — on the surface-tell axis this draft is clean.

## 5. Attention drift (moderate severity)

**"It used how many fish a boat caught per hour of effort as a stand-in for how many fish were actually still out there."** (para. 37) This is the one sentence in the chapter that has to be read twice. Everywhere else the prose is anchored in scene or in a person; here it's an abstract methodological explanation (catch-per-unit-effort as a stand-in for population) delivered in indirect, nested phrasing — "how many X... as a stand-in for how many Y" — right before it drops "recruitment overfishing" as an unglossed term of art. It's surrounded by strong material (the gauge/tank metaphor that follows rescues it), but for one sentence the letter voice goes quiet and a textbook voice fills in.

## 6. Trust (low-moderate severity)

One consistent voice throughout — the "I" writing to "Nate" never wavers, and the frame (recurring correspondent, callback to "the last chapter," promise of "next time") is well sustained.

The vulnerability is real in content (admitting he wanted the fishermen to be the villain) but slightly performed in delivery. The tell is that the admission pre-empts its own charge of being showy: "I'm telling you this not because it's a charming detail about my process but because it's a reflex almost everyone has" (para. 30). A person actually embarrassed doesn't usually narrate why their embarrassment isn't self-serving — that's a writer's move, defending the confession before the reader can object to it. It reads as sincere on first pass; a second read might feel it as engineered sincerity.

## 7. Factual side-eye (low severity)

- The Cabot detail — "sailors claiming you could slow a ship down just by sailing it through them" (para. 17) — is correctly hedged as a claim rather than asserted fact, but it's a well-known piece of fishery folklore of contested provenance. A reader who knows the historiography will clock the hedge as doing real work, not just style.
- "Pick the label you like" (para. 55), already flagged above under argument structure, will also read to a skeptical reader as an evasion dressed as generosity — declining to adjudicate a fight the chapter itself needs adjudicated to keep its two case studies structurally parallel.
- Everything else checked (Sinclair, Alverson, Finlayson, Marchak, Schelling, Hardin's 1998 retraction, the Amazon HQ2 dollar figures, the 2024 partial reopening) is internally consistent and reads as genuinely researched rather than invented — no other claims prompted suspicion on a single pass.
