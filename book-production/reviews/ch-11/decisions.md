# Ch-11 Decisions

**Gate 1 (2026-08-06, orchestrator as Showrunner proxy — see reviews/DEVIATIONS.md):**

- **Opening case: Wesley Autrey, New York subway, 2 January 2007.** He is the right opener precisely
  because his own account denies deliberating and names the trained pattern that fired instead
  (confined-space judgment from construction work). Carlos Arredondo (Boston, 2013) is the backup.
- **Sullenberger is DEMOTED to a supporting reference, and the reason matters.** The NTSB record
  complicates the "there was no time to think" reading: when investigators inserted a realistic
  thirty-five-second recognition delay into the simulator runs, the return-to-LaGuardia scenario
  crashed — which means Sullenberger made a compressed judgment among options, not no judgment at
  all. If the chapter uses him, the claim is **compressed deliberation, not eliminated deliberation**.
  Being the most famous case is a cost here, not a benefit; the reader has heard it.
- **Dark-side case REQUIRED: Kim Potter / Daunte Wright (2021).** A chapter arguing that responses
  can be deliberately installed owes the reader the fact that installed responses fire whether or not
  the situation warrants them. Handle at the confidence the record supports and without rhetoric.
- **Humility gap, flagged honestly:** none of the candidate cases dramatizes humility *in the moment*
  — they show courage, competence, calm. Humility appears in how the actors talk afterward (Autrey:
  "I don't feel like I did something spectacular"). The chapter must NOT imply the opening case
  demonstrates the keystone virtue; if humility is the keystone, that claim gets argued elsewhere.

**BINDING accuracy requirements:**
1. **Aristotle citation method:** Bekker numbers + a named public-domain translation, named in the
   endnote. The `Files/` copy is image-only and two OCR attempts failed (see research/FILES_INDEX.md).
   The genuine habituation passage is 1103a32–1103b2.
2. **Two misattributions are BANNED book-wide:** "habit is second nature" is **Cicero**; "we are what
   we repeatedly do; excellence, then, is not an act but a habit" is **Will Durant's 1926 paraphrase**
   of Aristotle, not Aristotle. The v1 project made the Durant error once already.
3. **Situationism reported honestly in both directions.** The chapter may claim neither that
   situationism collapsed nor that it held up. Stanford Prison and Milgram have taken real
   methodological damage; Doris's actual evidence base (Darley & Batson's Good Samaritan study —
   63% / 45% / 10% helping by hurry condition, verified against the 1973 original) is a more modest
   kind of study whose direct replication the researcher could not confirm either way. Say that.
4. **Lean into the concession.** The framework's answer to Doris — installed responses plus
   situation-selection — converges on his own "local traits" position rather than refuting it. POS-16
   already says as much. Present it as a concession the framework survives, not a victory.
