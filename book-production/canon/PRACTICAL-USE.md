# Can you actually use this on a political or economic question?

## The honest answer about the current specification

**No — not as written, and that is partly deliberate and partly a defect.**

The intro volume gives politics and economics two chapters and about ten thousand words. Both are
under an explicit instruction: *method only, no policy conclusions* (CHAPTER_BRIEFS Ch. 16–17,
CANON §7). Every specific — minimum wage, zoning, school choice, carbon pricing — is deferred to a
Volume II that does not exist yet.

**The deliberate part is right.** A reader who thinks you are wrong about the minimum wage should not
thereby discard the metaethics. Quarantining policy protects the core and is the only way a social
democrat and a libertarian can both use the same framework, which is the actual test of whether it is
a method rather than a conclusion wearing one.

**The defect is real too, and it is the mirror image.** A method that never produces a verdict is
indistinguishable from a method that cannot. If the framework is compatible with every arrangement, it
constrains nothing, and "apply the framework" becomes a way of dressing conclusions you already held.
The book currently risks buying wide appeal with vacuity.

**The fix is not a policy bundle. It is a constraint set.**

---

## What the framework actually forbids

This is the part that has been implicit and must become explicit. The framework is genuinely silent on
a great deal, and genuinely decisive about the following. Each one is a *veto*, not a preference, and
each traces to a position rather than to the author's politics.

**1 · Nothing may push people below the capability floor.** (POS-21, and since CK-15 this is a
legitimacy precondition, not a welfare preference.) Below the floor a person cannot exit, object, or
impose a cost on anyone — so the system's own claim to deserve obedience is false for them. This is
demanding. It rules out any arrangement that trades a permanent underclass for aggregate growth,
whatever the growth figures.

**2 · No institution may lack a removal mechanism.** (POS-19.) Democracy is justified here as the
means of getting rid of bad rulers, not as an oracle about technical questions. An arrangement that
performs well and cannot be removed fails this test *while performing well* — which is the whole
point, because you cannot tell in advance which competent government is the last one.

**3 · No policy may degrade the capacity to detect its own failure.** (POS-17.) This rules out the
entire family of "suspend normal process during the emergency" arguments in their standard form,
because the emergency is exactly when error-detection is most load-bearing and most likely to be
dismantled for convenience.

**4 · No coordination may be organised against an excluded group.** (POS-11, Axis 2.) This is the one
with the widest reach and the least partisan symmetry. It rules out cartels, rent-seeking coalitions,
and every arrangement whose efficiency comes from someone outside the bargain bearing the cost. Note
the warning from CK-14: mechanism design applied to an Axis 2 failure makes it *worse*, because better
alignment among the parties intensifies the harm to those outside.

**5 · No aggregate gain may breach the separateness constraints.** (POS-09.) People are not
resources to be drawn on for a total.

**6 · The epistemic commons may not be degraded.** (Axis 3.) A polity that cannot find out what is true
cannot correct anything, so this is upstream of everything else.

**These are not symmetric between left and right, and the book should not pretend they are.** Item 1
cuts hard against growth-first arguments; item 3 cuts hard against emergency-powers arguments wherever
they come from; item 4 cuts against incumbent industries and against protectionist coalitions alike.
Any honest application will land on positions that annoy both sides, which is evidence the constraints
are doing work rather than laundering priors.

## The procedure, which is what "practically usable" actually means

A reader should be able to run this on a live question tonight. Four steps, in order.

**Step 1 — Diagnose the axis, because the tool depends on it.**
Is this a coordination failure (individually reasonable choices, collectively bad), a circle failure
(coordination working *against* someone outside it), or an epistemic failure (harm from false belief)?
Most real cases are combinations, and the dominant axis determines the instrument. Applying mechanism
design to a circle failure actively worsens it.

**Step 2 — Run the constraint check.** Does any option breach the six vetoes above? If so it is out,
and it is out regardless of its benefits. This is where the framework does its real work: it does not
tell you which of the permitted options to pick, and it removes some from the table entirely.

**Step 3 — Audit the empirical premises.** Almost every political disagreement that feels moral is
actually a disagreement about facts wearing moral clothes. Write down what your position depends on
factually, and how confident the literature actually supports being — not how confident you feel. The
book's own Ch. 2 diagnoses the failure mode here: confidence tightens exactly where interest is
engaged.

**Step 4 — Run the divergence test.** Could a person applying this method faithfully, with different
but defensible empirical priors, reach a different answer? **If the answer is no, be suspicious.** For
most contested policy questions the answer should be yes, and a method that always delivers your prior
is not a method. If you find yourself never diverging from your own side, the framework is being used
as decoration.

---

## What Volume II owes

The intro volume can carry the constraint set and the procedure. Practical use at policy resolution
needs the second volume, and its structure follows from the above rather than from a topic list:

- Each policy area worked through the four steps end to end, with the empirical premises exposed and
  rated — not asserted.
- For each, an explicit statement of **what the framework rules out** and **what it leaves open**, kept
  separate. The second list should be longer.
- At least one case where the author's own position loses to the constraint set, identified as such.
  Without it the volume is a bundle with a method stapled on.
- Confidence ratings on empirical claims matched to the literature rather than to conviction, with the
  minimum-wage claim as the standing example of how the previous draft got this wrong.

---

## The one-line summary for a reader

*It will not tell you how to vote. It will tell you which arguments are unavailable to you, make you
write down what your position depends on factually, and show you when someone who disagrees is
reasoning as well as you are.*

That is less than a political programme and more than most frameworks deliver, and the book should
claim exactly that much.
