# Welfare — what the framework says a life going well consists in

The value theory has been the least-built part of the system: POS-06 says only valence is
intrinsically valuable and that autonomy, competence, connection, understanding and meaning are
*conditions* of it. That is a position sketch, not a defence. Three rival accounts have been standing
outside unaddressed, and one of them has an argument that goes at the axiom itself.

Everything below is anchored in a case, because the abstraction is where this material goes to die.

---

## The three answers, and a man refusing morphine

Parfit lays out the standard taxonomy in *Reasons and Persons* (Appendix I, p. 493): **Hedonistic**
theories say what is best for you is what makes your life happiest; **Desire-Fulfilment** theories say
it is what best fulfils your desires; **Objective List** theories say certain things are good for you
whether or not you want them.

Start with the case that makes the disagreement concrete. Near the end of his life, with cancer of the
jaw, Freud refused pain-killing drugs. He preferred, as Parfit reports the case from Griffin, to think
in torment rather than to be confusedly euphoric (p. 494).

Take the three theories to that room:

- **Hedonism** has to say Freud made himself worse off. Euphoria is the more pleasant mental state;
  he declined it.
- **Desire theory** says he made himself better off, because his life went as he preferred.
- **Objective list** says it depends whether clear thought is on the list.

The framework has to say something here, and until now it has not. Worse: read strictly, POS-06 puts
it with hedonism, and hedonism gives the answer almost nobody believes.

---

## The correction: the framework is asymmetric, and always was

Here is the argument that should have been worrying us. Parfit again, same page:

> Narrow Hedonists assume, falsely, that pleasure and pain are two distinctive kinds of experience.

His examples: the pleasure of satisfying an intense thirst, of listening to music, of solving an
intellectual problem, of reading a tragedy, of knowing that one's child is happy. **These do not share
a distinctive common quality.** What they share, he argues, is a relation to our desires — all
pleasures, when experienced, are wanted.

If that is right for pleasure, "positive valence" is not one phenomenal property with amounts. It is a
label over a heterogeneous pile, and what unifies the pile is *wanting*, which hands the whole thing to
the attitudinal theorists CANON §2.4 already concedes are live.

**And the argument is far weaker for the negative pole — where we have evidence Parfit did not use.**

Pain asymbolia. The patient in Ch. 5 reports the pin, describes it as painful, and does not mind it —
laughs, offers more of her body to be pricked. If the badness of pain were *constituted* by the
aversion to it, a patient with no aversion would be a patient with no pain. She isn't. She has the
pain and lacks the minding, which is exactly the dissociation an attitudinal theory says cannot
happen.

There is no comparably clean dissociation on the positive side. Nobody presents with intact pleasure
and absent wanting in a way that isolates a felt quality of goodness.

**So the framework's confidence should be asymmetric, and the axiom already was.** It says *the
suffering of a conscious being matters*. It has never said pleasure is the only good, and the book has
been reading a symmetry into it that the words do not contain.

**The position, stated properly:**

- **About suffering: phenomenal realism, at MODERATE confidence.** The awfulness is a felt property of
  the state, met in acquaintance, dissociable from the aversion. This is what POS-01 defends and what
  the asymbolia evidence supports.
- **About flourishing: goods pluralism, at lower confidence.** A life going well involves autonomy,
  competence, connection, understanding and meaning. These are not one substance in different amounts,
  and the book should stop implying they are convertible into a single quantity.

**This is not negative utilitarianism.** Flourishing matters; we are simply less certain what it
consists in than we are that agony is bad, and the confidence levels should show it. A framework whose
whole method is calibration should not claim the same certainty about the good life that it claims
about a burn.

**It revises a verdict.** `VERDICTS.md` recorded the experience machine as a bitten bullet: under
strict value monism a machine delivering equivalent sustained valence would be permissible to enter,
and our objection was merely a prediction. Under the asymmetric position that is no longer forced. The
machine removes autonomy, connection and understanding, and those are not stand-ins for a valence
quantity it could supply by other means. **I am recording this as a revision rather than quietly
fixing it**, because the earlier verdict was published in the same repository and the difference
matters.

## Against preferentism — three rooms

The strongest rival, and the one the goal names: suffering as a preference ordering, welfare as
getting what you want.

**Room one: the stranger on the train.** Parfit's own case (p. 494). You meet a stranger with what is
believed to be a fatal disease, you want them cured, you never meet again, and — unknown to you — they
recover. On an unrestricted desire theory this is *good for you*; your life went better. It plainly
didn't. Parfit rejects the theory on exactly this ground, and so do we.

**Room two: the woman who no longer wants what she is denied.** This is the case that does the real
damage. Preferences adapt to deprivation. Serene Khader's account defines adaptive preferences as
deficits in the capacity to lead a flourishing life that are *causally related to deprivation* — and
she is careful, against some liberal framings, not to treat the person as merely duped or lacking
autonomy.

Take it seriously as a test. If welfare just is preference satisfaction, then a person whose
expectations have contracted to fit a narrow life is *doing fine*, and the contraction itself is
invisible to the theory — indeed the cheapest way to raise welfare is to teach people to want less.
Any theory with that implication has lost contact with what we wanted a welfare theory for.

**This is also where the framework's own machinery earns its keep.** The capability floor (POS-21) is
capability-based precisely because capability survives preference adaptation: what a person is *able*
to do and be does not shrink just because their wanting did. Khader's account and our floor are doing
the same work, and Ch. 17 should say so.

**Room three: the man who wants not to be deceived.** Parfit's case again (p. 494–5): you strongly want
not to be deceived. On preference-hedonism, only introspectible features count, so a deception you
never discover costs you nothing. On his Success Theory it makes your life worse.

Our answer follows the asymmetry: the deception does not make your experience worse, and it does wrong
you — but the wrong is *not* welfare loss. It is a violation of the coordination layer, of what you were
owed by someone who took your trust. Keeping those apart is a feature. A theory that has to run every
wrong through the welfare ledger ends up either inflating welfare or excusing betrayal.

**What preferentism gets right, and we must not lose.** Back to Freud. His preference for clarity over
comfort was *authoritative* about his own remaining weeks. Any theory that tells him he was mistaken
about his own life is not describing a life.

**The resolution:** preferences are **evidence about, and authority over, one's own goods — not the
constituents of welfare.** Freud is not making euphoria bad by declining it. He is telling us, with
better standing than anyone else has, what those weeks were for. That is why the framework's
institutions default to what people say they want (they know things we don't) while its floor does not
(preferences adapt, and a floor that adapts with them is not a floor).

## Flourishing, and how far we really are from an objective list

The honest position is that we are closer to an objective list than the book has implied, and the
difference is worth stating exactly rather than overstating.

An objective list *posits* goods. We *derive* them: autonomy, competence, connection, understanding
and meaning are held to be robustly necessary conditions of sustained positive valence in beings
constituted like us. That keeps the single-axiom claim, which is one of the framework's genuine
selling points.

**But the derivation is an empirical bet, and it must be paid for.** If the psychological literature
did not support the claim that these conditions are load-bearing for a life going well over time, the
position would collapse into an objective list with extra steps — and the extra steps would be a
disguise. So the book owes real evidence here (self-determination theory is the obvious place for
autonomy, competence and relatedness) and owes an honest statement of what would falsify it: a
population sustaining high positive valence over a life while lacking these conditions.

**And the asymmetry applies here too.** We are more confident that these conditions matter than we are
about their exchange rate. The framework should refuse to price connection against understanding, and
say plainly that refusing is not evasion but the actual state of the evidence.

## What this changes

1. **POS-06 is rewritten** as an asymmetric position: phenomenal realism about suffering, goods
   pluralism at lower confidence about flourishing. The "only valence is intrinsically valuable"
   formulation goes; it read as symmetric hedonism and imported hedonism's worst results.
2. **The experience-machine verdict is revised** and the revision is recorded.
3. **Preferences get a defined role** — evidential and authoritative, not constitutive — which explains
   why institutions defer to stated preferences while the floor does not.
4. **The capability floor gains its philosophical partner:** capability survives preference
   adaptation, which is why the floor is stated in capabilities.
5. **Goods pluralism acquires a falsification condition**, without which it is a posited list wearing
   a derivation.
