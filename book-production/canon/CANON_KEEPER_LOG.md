Affects: Ch. 5 (must name Street and her pain-specific argument, not just wave at "the Darwinian dilemma"), Ch. 7 (tiers).

## Strengthening pass, 2026-08-27 (CK-9 … CK-16)
Full argument in `canon/STRENGTHENING-2026-08.md`. These are canon changes, not suggestions.

**CK-9 — Value and reasons are split (POS-04 rebuilt).** The coordination layer was being asked to
establish both that others' suffering has value and that it gives you reason to act. It cannot do the
first and does not need to: phenomenal value realism delivers agent-neutral VALUE directly, because a
badness constitutive of a state is not indexed to an observer (this is CK-8's no-tracking move,
carried through). The coordination layer supplies REASONS. Against the durably-advantaged egoist the
book no longer claims a refutation: it claims he must hold that badness varies with whose it is,
which his own acquaintance-based epistemology overturns whenever he is himself in pain. Sidgwick's
dualism survives and is named as surviving. Affects: Ch. 5 (the value half is now its to state), Ch. 6
(the reasons half; the egoist section rewritten), POS-01, POS-04.

**CK-10 — The circle argument's mechanism is relocated (POS-05).** Instability was located in pressure
from the excluded, which is why it failed for beings who can never reciprocate. It now sits with the
INCLUDERS: a boundary must be maintained by a criterion, and every criterion ever used either cuts
into the in-group (reciprocity, rationality, capacity — all possessed in degrees and unevenly inside
the line) or is admitted arbitrary (species). Second mechanism: arbitrary exclusions carry ongoing
maintenance costs — ideology, ritual, policed edge cases — that inclusive lines do not, and
high-maintenance equilibria lose under drift. Reaches marginal cases. Affects: Ch. 6.

**CK-11 — Stability is not rightness, book-wide (new POS-23).** Three claims that were being used
interchangeably must now be labelled wherever they appear: STRUCTURAL (this is a stable equilibrium —
predictive, no normative content), PRUDENTIAL (you'd accept it under uncertainty — doesn't reach the
entrenched), NORMATIVE (suffering is bad irrespective of whose — from the axiom). Where only the
structural claim is available, the text says so. Affects: every chapter that argues from equilibrium.

**CK-12 — Standing becomes the sixth override condition, consulted first (POS-13).** The gates filtered
reasoning but never self-appointment; as written the architecture was satisfiable by anyone anywhere
with a strong opinion. An agent must hold CAUSAL standing (in the chain), ROLE standing (the position
assigns it), or LAST-ACTOR standing (nobody better placed in the time available). The book's own cases
already turn on this silently — Buxtun inside the PHS, Cooper in internal audit, Thompson over the
village. Affects: Ch. 9, Ch. 10 (frozen — flagged for the Showrunner, see below).

**CK-13 — Moral repair enters the framework (new POS-24).** The system had nothing on what is owed
AFTER wrongdoing, while Ch. 2 already establishes that forgiveness is load-bearing (FRIEDMAN's
permanent retaliation finishes last among nice rules). Repair is the re-entry route: acknowledgment,
restitution, costly change, and — the ethical crux — the wronged party's standing to withhold
forgiveness. Forgiveness is a coordination benefit, never a debt owed by the person harmed. Affects:
Ch. 13 (personal), Ch. 16 (institutional).

**CK-14 — The three axes gain an interaction theory (POS-11).** Named combinations: coordination
success weaponised by circle failure (mechanism design makes it WORSE — the toolkit must carry this
warning); epistemic failure as circle maintenance (ideology's function); coordination failure obscured
by manufactured fog (the modern default). Diagnostic rule: identify the dominant axis before selecting
a tool. Affects: Ch. 1, Ch. 6, Ch. 16.

**CK-15 — The capability floor is a legitimacy precondition, not a welfare threshold (POS-21).** Below
it a person cannot exit, object, or impose a cost, so error-correction primacy (POS-17) — the system's
own account of why its institutions deserve obedience — is simply false for them. The floor stops
being tradeable at the margin. Confidence rises to STRONG. Affects: Ch. 16, Ch. 17.

**CK-16 — Aggregation priority rule, and bounded moral uncertainty.** (a) Floor lexically prior up to
the floor; prioritarian weighting above it; separateness constraints bound both — and the book names a
case where this refuses a large aggregate gain rather than pretending the structure is costless.
(b) New POS-25: expected-value reasoning under moral uncertainty informs deliberation but never by
itself clears the override gates, which is the brake against fanaticism. Affects: Ch. 6, Ch. 17.
