# CANON — The Framework, Revised

**Purpose:** the single source of truth for what the framework claims. Written so that no agent needs the 1,325-page source manuscript. Where this file and the source disagree, **this file wins** — it incorporates revisions made after critical review.

Confidence ratings and known vulnerabilities are in `POSITIONS.md`. Nothing in the book may state a position at higher confidence than its rating there.

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

Most suffering is produced by coordination failure rather than malice; morality is the technology that escapes it; that technology has to be grounded in something real, and the only thing real enough is the felt badness of suffering itself; and the right way to run it in a biased brain is committed rules with a narrowly and procedurally gated override.

---

## 2. Metaethics

### 2.1 The position, named

**Phenomenal value realism.** The badness of suffering is not a further fact appended to the experience; it is constitutive of it. What it is to be in pain is to be in a state whose felt character is bad. Moral epistemology is therefore partly a matter of introspective acquaintance rather than inference from evolved evaluative attitudes.

Literature: Sinhababu, *Humean Nature* (the phenomenal introspection argument); Kahane, "Pain, Dislike and Experience"; Bramble on the distinctive feeling theory; Crisp, *Reasons and the Good*.

**Do not use the label "Constructivist Realism" in this book.** Korsgaard and Street already own "constructivism" in metaethics and both reach conclusions this framework rejects. Using it invites a dismissal in the first review.

### 2.2 Why this position and not the old three-layer defence

The prior draft claimed three independent grounding layers — reflective equilibrium, performative incoherence, inference to the best explanation — and argued that an opponent must defeat all three. That claim is false and must not appear in the book. Layers 1 and 3 cite the same four facts in different syntax, and the error theorist's reply to Layer 2 ("that's a fact about your psychology, not about the universe") defeats all three identically. One objection reaches every layer.

### 2.3 The degradation tiers — use this instead

The book's metaethical claim is that **the practical content is invariant across the live space of metaethical positions.**

- **Tier 1 — phenomenal realism true.** Naturalist realism grounded in the intrinsic badness of suffering. Full strength.
- **Tier 2 — illusionism true.** Representationalism: the relevant property is representational content that is intrinsically avoidance-generating. Weaker metaphysics, identical normative output.
- **Tier 3 — strong anti-realism true.** The framework degrades into quasi-realism (Blackburn) plus a coordination theory. You lose "constrained by objective reality"; you keep every institutional and practical conclusion.

This is both more honest and dialectically stronger than the three-pillar framing, and it is what lets a religious reader, a Buddhist, and an error theorist all use the rest of the book.

### 2.4 Objections that must be handled in text

| Objection | Source | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Queerness — intrinsically prescriptive properties don't exist | Mackie | Phenomenal consciousness already violates Mackie's ontological expectations independently; the argument was a priori and the a priori has a poor record here |
| Open question — "it feels bad, but is it bad?" | Moore | Fails against a posteriori identity claims (water/H₂O); this is that kind of claim, not conceptual analysis |
| Darwinian dilemma | Street | Targets evolved *evaluative attitudes*. Acquaintance with a phenomenal property is not a debunkable tracking relation. **Do not use the thermometer analogy from the prior draft** — it answers the wrong half |
| Illusionism | Frankish, Dennett | Tier 2 |
| Attitudinal theories — badness is the dislike, not the sensation | Heathwood, Feldman | Concede it's live; show the framework survives on either reading |

## 3. The axiom and the scope

**Axiom:** the suffering of a conscious being matters. Chosen, not derived. This is the only place normativity enters.

**Impartiality is NOT derived from the axiom.** The prior draft tried to get from "my suffering matters to me" to "suffering matters" by shifting the burden onto the egoist. That fails — the indexical egoist has a principled reply (Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason), and the book must not pretend otherwise.

**The derivation splits across the two foundations:**

1. **The axiom supplies the currency.** Valence is what has value.
2. **The coordination layer supplies the impartiality.** Agents in an indefinitely iterated game, uncertain of their future bargaining position, converge on norms that count valence impartially, because partial norms are not stable equilibria among agents who cannot guarantee staying on the favoured side. (Gauthier, Binmore, Skyrms — but note this version avoids Gauthier's fatal problem, because the base is *value* rather than preference satisfaction.)

**For beings who cannot reciprocate** — infants, animals, the severely disabled, future people — use the boundary argument:

> Arbitrary moral-circle boundaries are unstable Schelling points. Once valence is the currency, every boundary short of "all valence" needs a defensible line. Every line ever proposed — tribe, race, nation, sex, species, cognitive threshold — has eroded under pressure, because none is a natural focal point and each is exploitable by anyone with an interest in redrawing it. "All valence" is the only boundary that doesn't erode. Circle expansion is equilibrium selection, not moral fashion.

This is the framework's most original argument and should be given its own chapter (Ch. 6).

## 4. Value theory

**Value monism, goods pluralism.** Only valence is intrinsically valuable. Autonomy, competence, connection, understanding, and meaning are robustly necessary *conditions* of sustained positive valence in beings constituted like us — empirically load-bearing, not axiomatically independent. This preserves the single-axiom claim.

**Do not use the "six-dimensional value vector" from the prior draft.** It was asserted without derivation and it has no aggregation rule, so it doesn't generate verdicts.

**Aggregation structure** (this is what blocks the classic counterexamples, not incommensurability):

- **Prioritarianism.** The moral weight of a valence increment rises as the recipient's baseline falls. Blocks the utility monster: the monster's marginal gains are weighted down. (Parfit, "Equality or Priority?"; Arneson.)
- **Sufficientarian threshold.** A capability floor, lexically prior to aggregate maximisation below the line. (Crisp; Nussbaum; Frankfurt.)
- **Separateness-of-persons constraints.** The transplant case is blocked by a rights structure derived from coordination stability, not by welfare arithmetic.
- **Repugnant conclusion.** The book takes a position and defends it rather than deferring. Default: person-affecting restriction, with the costs acknowledged. Alternatives (critical-level, or biting the bullet à la Huemer/Tännsjö) are acceptable if the Showrunner prefers, but silence is not.

## 5. The diagnosis — three axes, not one

The prior draft treated coordination failure ("Moloch") as the whole enemy. It isn't, and the toolkit didn't map onto the diagnosis. Use three axes:

**Axis 1 — Coordination failure.** Individually rational choices, collectively catastrophic. Commons tragedies, arms races, races to the bottom, stag hunts. **Tool: mechanism design.**

**Axis 2 — Circle failure.** Successful coordination *against* excluded parties. Slavery, genocide, cartels, conquest are coordination *victories* for the in-group. Mechanism design makes these worse — better incentive alignment among perpetrators intensifies the harm. **Tool: rights, constitutional constraint, circle-expansion arguments.**

**Axis 3 — Epistemic failure.** Suffering caused by false belief, independent of both. **Tool: the epistemic commons, free expression, forecasting institutions, institutional truth-seeking.**

Every instrument in the book now has a diagnostic home. This typology is a genuine contribution and should be presented as one.

## 6. The decision architecture

### 6.1 Mode A — the default

Follow pre-committed rules and cultivated virtues. Justification: rules are bias-resistant, cognitively cheap, predictability-producing (which is what makes you trustworthy), and they encode base rates your inside view can't access. Even ideal consequentialists would adopt rules, because a rule-follower can be trusted in ways a calculator cannot.

**Chesterton's fence:** an inherited institution is *evidence*. Revision requires understanding why the rule exists first. This is not traditionalism — rules get revised — but "I can't see the point of this" is a fact about you before it's a fact about the fence.

### 6.2 Mode B — the override

Five substantive conditions: catastrophic irreversible harm from rule-following; harm attributable to the rule rather than merely coincident; last resort with alternatives genuinely exhausted; articulable to a skeptical third party who does not share your interests; self-interest absent or explicitly corrected for.

Three procedural gates, applied *before* the conditions are consulted: a bright-line trigger check (imminent large-scale loss of life / irreversible systemic damage / institutional capture that disables normal error-correction); a mandatory deliberation period; and mandatory documented post-hoc review regardless of outcome.

**The procedural gating is the framework's single most original contribution.** Most threshold deontologies stop at substantive conditions. Nobody else treats the agent as an unreliable evaluator of their own case and fixes it architecturally.

### 6.3 The false-negative side — NEW, must be in the book

Every condition and gate above filters false positives — overriding when you shouldn't. The book must also address **false negatives** — complying when you shouldn't. Milgram, My Lai, and every institutional cover-up are false-negative failures, and the prior draft had nothing to say to someone sitting inside one.

Additions:
- **Expected-cost calibration by domain.** Private life with reversible stakes: heavy anti-override bias is right. Captured institution facing irreversible harm: it inverts.
- **Role-relative thresholds.** A physician, a soldier, a civil servant and a private citizen have different override profiles, because their rules have different justificatory bases and different capture risks.
- **Fix the whistleblower contradiction.** Gate 1(c) opens the override for institutional capture; the deliberation period and documented review then bite hardest in exactly that case. Allow sealed or delayed documentation, and exempt time-sensitive capture from the delay.

### 6.4 Virtues

Firmware: installed response patterns the architecture runs on. Epistemic — curiosity, relinquishment, lightness, evenness, steel-manning, empiricism, simplicity, humility. Instrumental — effortful improvement, precision, scholarship, the void. **Humility is the keystone; without it every other virtue becomes a weapon.**

In the book these are prose, not a list of twelve. Introduce them where they do work rather than cataloguing them.

## 7. Applications (compressed — one chapter each)

**Institutions.** Error-correction primacy: design for the ability to detect and reverse mistakes, not for efficiency or for being right the first time. Separation of powers, federalism, subsidiarity (decisions at the lowest competent level), antifragility. Democracy is justified as an error-correction mechanism, not as an oracle — it is good at removing bad rulers and bad at technical questions.

**Markets.** Hayek's knowledge problem: prices carry dispersed information no planner can assemble. Markets fail on externalities, public goods, information asymmetry and market power. Pigovian correction where measurable; Ostrom-style community governance where the commons is local and the users can monitor each other; state provision for genuine public goods. Sen/Nussbaum capability floor as the redistributive commitment.

**Rule for both chapters:** present the *method*, not the policy bundle. Specific policy conclusions (minimum wage, school choice, zoning, carbon pricing) go to Volume II with their empirical premises exposed. The introduction must demonstrate that two readers with different defensible empirical priors can apply the method faithfully and diverge — and it must show what evidence would settle it.

## 8. What the framework does not solve

Must appear in the Coda, in the author's own voice:

- **Population ethics.** Every welfarist theory bleeds here.
- **The no-self problem.** If there is no persistent subject — Buddhist anātman, or Parfit's reductionism about personal identity — whose suffering matters, and across what boundaries does aggregation run? This is the sharpest live threat to a valence-grounded theory and it is not fully answered.
- **Consciousness.** The framework grounds value on conscious experience without a verified theory of consciousness. Operating rule: under uncertainty, err toward inclusion.
- **Free will.** Compatibilism is assumed as sufficient for moral practice. This affects judgment more than evaluation.
- **Our own blindness.** Every past moral framework was blind to something its successors could see. This one is too.
- **The author's own priors.** Name at least one place where they probably distorted the argument.
