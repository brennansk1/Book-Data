# Verdicts — the framework run against the standard cases

A moral system is judged by what it says, not by how it describes itself. This runs the amended
machinery (strengthening pass + rival audit) against the battery every system is tested on. Two of
these produced bullets we have to bite, and they are printed rather than smoothed.

Machinery in play: the axiom and phenomenal value realism (POS-01/03); bounded aggregation —
prioritarian above a lexically prior floor with separateness constraints bounding both
(POS-07/08/09); the standing rules (POS-12); the override — six conditions, standing consulted first,
three procedural gates (POS-13/14); goods pluralism (POS-06).

---

## Transplant — surgeon kills one healthy patient to save five

**Forbidden**, and for two independent reasons, which is the mark of a case the system handles rather
than survives.

First, the separateness constraint (POS-09): the one is *used* as the means. Second, the override
never opens — the trigger asks for catastrophic harm **attributable to the rule**, and the five are
dying of their diseases, not of the prohibition on killing patients. The rule is not what is killing
them.

Note that no appeal to intuition was required, and no doctrine of double effect.

## Trolley — divert onto one to save five

**Permitted.** Standing: you are at the lever, causally in the chain (CK-12). The harm is a side
effect of diverting a threat, not a means to saving anyone — remove the one from the track and the
five are still saved, which is precisely what fails in Transplant. Bounded aggregation then favours
five over one.

**Why this matters:** the framework explains the trolley/transplant asymmetry *structurally* — through
the used-as-means constraint — rather than by importing the doctrine of double effect or by reporting
that most people feel differently about the two.

## The murderer at the door

**Lie.** All six conditions clear: imminent irreversible harm; you hold standing (you are the one
asked); the harm follows directly from telling the truth; no gentler route; the reasoning is
articulable to a skeptical third party who does not share your interests; no self-interest in play.

This is the case that breaks Kant, and the framework should use it that way in Ch. 9 — not as a
gotcha, but as the clearest demonstration that an absolute rule with no override is not a livable
architecture.

## The drowning child, and Singer's extension

**Rescue the child** — trivial cost, last-actor standing, no competing claim.

**Resist the extension** to a continuous personal obligation over all comparable distant suffering.
Per POS-27, the standing rules bound the ordinary personal demand, and the heavy obligations run
through institutions that raise the floor.

**Bullet acknowledged:** this is the comfortable answer, and comfort is evidence of motivated
reasoning. POS-27 prints the weakness — routing obligation through institutions suits the
comfortable, and where the institutions do not exist or have been captured, we have not yet said what
a person owes in the meantime. That is the situation most readers are actually in.

## The ticking bomb

CANON §6.2 rejects it, and the amended machinery shows *why* — and the why is uncomfortable.

Standing is satisfied (the interrogator holds role standing). The trigger may clear. What actually
blocks it is **last resort** and **articulability**, and both are *empirical*: torture's reliability
as an information-extraction method is poor, and a skeptical third party holding the literature will
not accept the reasoning. The institutional gate adds the rest — an exception, once available, gets
used far outside the case that justified it.

**Bullet, printed:** the rejection is therefore contingent, not absolute. If torture were reliable in
a genuine ticking-bomb case, the gates could in principle clear. The framework does not have — and by
its own architecture cannot have — an exceptionless prohibition here. What it has instead is a
procedure that has never in practice been satisfied and a strong empirical claim that it will not be.
Saying this plainly is better than pretending to an absolutism the architecture does not support.

## The utility monster

**Blocked** by prioritarianism (POS-07): the monster's marginal gains are weighted down as its
baseline rises. No appeal to incommensurability is needed, which is what the old six-dimensional
value vector was doing and could not justify.

## The repugnant conclusion

**Avoided** by the wide person-affecting view with conditional reasons (POS-10, CK-18): mere addition
of lives barely worth living is not an improvement, because reasons to confer well-being are
conditional on existence.

**Cost, per Arrhenius:** every population axiology accepts some strongly counterintuitive conclusion.
Ours accepts that certain very large populations cannot be ranked against certain small ones as
cleanly as a total view would. The book names this rather than claiming escape.

## The experience machine

**A genuine bullet, and the sharpest one here.** POS-06 holds that only valence is intrinsically
valuable and that autonomy, competence, connection, understanding and meaning are *conditions* of
sustained positive valence rather than independent goods. Taken strictly: a machine that truly
delivered equivalent sustained valence would have to be permissible to enter.

The framework's real objection is therefore **empirical, not moral** — the machine would not deliver
it, because the goods that sustain positive valence over a life are precisely what the machine
removes. That is a prediction, and predictions can be wrong.

We should print this rather than reach for a value-pluralism the axiom does not license. It is the
clearest place where the single-axiom commitment costs something, and a reader who wants to reject the
framework here has a real handle to pull.

## Promises to the dead, and other non-experiential harms

**Binding**, and the route is instructive: not through valence — no one's experience is affected — but
through the coordination layer. Promise-keeping is an institution whose value comes from its
reliability, and reliability that lapses when the promisee cannot check is not reliability.

This is the clearest demonstration that the two-source structure (CK-9: value from the axiom, reasons
from coordination) does real work rather than being an accounting convenience. A pure welfarist has to
strain here; we do not.

---

## What the battery revealed

Two bullets that must be printed: **the ticking-bomb prohibition is contingent rather than absolute**,
and **the experience-machine objection is empirical rather than moral**. Both follow from commitments
the framework makes deliberately — the procedural architecture in the first case, the single axiom in
the second. A system that produced no bullets under this battery would be one that had not been run
against it honestly.

One strength worth claiming: the trolley/transplant asymmetry, the murderer at the door, the utility
monster and promises to the dead are all handled by *machinery already in the system for other
reasons*, with no case-specific patches. That is the property that distinguishes a framework from a
collection of verdicts.
