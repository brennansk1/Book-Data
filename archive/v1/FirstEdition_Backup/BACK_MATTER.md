## Key Terms Glossary

*Definitions of technical terms as used in this book. Page numbers to be added during typesetting.*

**Asymmetric caution** — Under uncertainty about consciousness or moral status, err toward inclusion rather than exclusion, because the cost of wrongly excluding a conscious being is greater than the cost of wrongly including a non-conscious one. (CH-05)

**Bayesian updating** — Holding beliefs as probability distributions and revising them in light of new evidence, weighted by prior probability and likelihood. The formal implementation of epistemic duty. (CH-02)

**Capability floor** — The minimum set of real freedoms (Sen) that all persons deserve as a matter of justice, regardless of market outcomes. Extends the safety-net position beyond pure insurance to cover predictable disadvantage. (CH-28)

**Chesterton Fence** — The principle that inherited moral rules should not be destroyed until the person proposing destruction can explain why the rule exists. Applied to moral heuristics in CH-09.

**Complex value vector** — The six-dimensional model of human flourishing: hedonic tone, autonomy, competence, connection, understanding, meaning. Value is not a scalar; it cannot be summed into a single number. (CH-03)

**Constructivist Realism** — The framework's metaethical position. Values are constructed by conscious agents but constrained by objective features of reality (biology, game theory, physics of consciousness). (CH-01)

**Dual-process ethics** — The decision architecture: Mode A (rule-following by default) + Mode B (consequentialist override under strict conditions). (CH-09, CH-10)

**Epistemic duty** — The moral obligation to seek truth, because false beliefs produce unnecessary suffering. Not merely an intellectual preference. (CH-02)

**Error-correction primacy** — The political principle that the best system is not the one that produces the best outcomes directly, but the one that corrects errors fastest. (CH-18)

**Eudaimonia** — Aristotelian term for flourishing or living well. The framework's complex value vector is a descendant of this concept. (CH-03, CH-42)

**Hayekian knowledge test** — Before endorsing any policy that substitutes administrative judgment for market prices, ask: does the policy-maker have access to the knowledge needed? (CH-24)

**Informed automaticity** — Stage 3 of moral development: automatic responses backed by reasons that can be articulated when needed. Not regression to instinct but completion of reflection. (CH-12)

**Mechanism design** — The engineering discipline of designing institutions and incentive structures that produce desired outcomes from self-interested agents. (CH-08)

**Mode A** — The default decision mode: follow pre-committed rules and cultivated virtues. Bias-resistant, cognitively cheap, coordination-enabling. (CH-09)

**Mode B** — The override mode: explicit consequentialist calculation, invoked only when all five conditions are met (catastrophic harm, attributable to rule, last resort, articulable, self-interest absent). (CH-10)

**Moloch** — Metaphor (from Ginsberg/Alexander) for the god of coordination failure: the logic of bad Nash equilibria that produces unnecessary suffering from individually rational choices. (CH-06)

**Performative incoherence** — A claim that undermines itself in the act of being asserted. Used to defend the valence axiom: denying that suffering matters while caring about one's own suffering is performatively incoherent. (CH-01, CH-03)

**Pigovian fit test** — Does the proposed intervention actually match the market failure it claims to address? Tax externalities, subsidize positive externalities, disclose asymmetric information, break up monopolies. (CH-24, CH-27)

**Position Registry** — The self-correction mechanism: every substantive position is registered with confidence and vulnerability ratings, and can be formally swapped when a better position is found. (Positions/POSITION_REGISTRY.md)

**Schelling point** — A coordination equilibrium that agents converge on without explicit communication, because it is salient, obvious, or traditional. Rights function as Schelling points in the framework. (CH-07, CH-19)

**Scout mindset** — Galef's term for the epistemic posture of treating beliefs as maps to be refined rather than fortresses to be defended. The core epistemic virtue. (CH-02)

**Shadow of the future** — Axelrod's parameter w: the probability that agents will interact again. When w is high, cooperation stabilizes; when low, defection dominates. (CH-07)

**Subsidiarity** — Decisions should be made at the lowest level of governance competent to handle them. Local knowledge (metis) is destroyed by unnecessary centralization. (CH-17)

**Symmetric diagnostic** — In economic policy, evaluate market failure and government failure at the same level of rigor before concluding that intervention improves welfare. (CH-24)

**Valence** — The suffering-to-flourishing spectrum of conscious experience. A physical property of conscious systems, not a projected preference. The foundation of the framework's normative claims. (CH-03)

**Valence axiom** — "The suffering of a conscious being matters." The framework's minimal normative starting point, defended as the hardest-to-deny axiom available. (CH-01, CH-03)

## Notes on the Position Registry

This book's architecture includes a formal self-correction mechanism called the **Position Registry** (described in Chapter 1 and maintained at `Positions/POSITION_REGISTRY.md` in the project repository).

Every substantive claim the system makes is registered with:
- A **confidence rating** (AXIOM, STRONG, MODERATE, PROVISIONAL, EXPLORATORY)
- A **vulnerability rating** (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL)
- The best known objection and current rebuttal

When a better position is found through research, argument, or reader feedback, the old position is formally deprecated with a full explanation, the new position is installed, and all affected chapters are revised. This is what makes the system self-correcting rather than dogmatic.

Readers who identify weaknesses in specific positions are encouraged to engage with the arguments and, if their objections are stronger than the current rebuttals, to propose position swaps. The system is designed to get better over time, and it can only do so if its weaknesses are exposed rather than protected.

The Position Index above shows where each position stands as of this edition. Future editions will reflect any swaps that have occurred since publication.
