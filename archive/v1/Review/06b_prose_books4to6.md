# Phase 6b: Prose and Editorial Polish -- Books IV-VI (CH-13 through CH-30)

**Date:** 2026-04-05
**Scope:** Chapters 13-30 (Book IV: Personal Ethics, Book V: Political Philosophy, Book VI: Economic Philosophy)
**Criteria:** Redundancy, paragraph length, weak transitions, hedging excess, jargon without payoff

---

## Summary of Approach

Each chapter was read in full and edited surgically for the five target criteria. Substance of arguments, direct quotes, section headings, and epigraphs were left untouched. Edits focused on tightening prose, eliminating redundant throat-clearing, sharpening hedged language, and improving flow.

## General Observations

1. **Books IV-VI are well-written overall.** The prose is clear, the arguments are well-structured, and the chapters flow naturally. Most edits were minor tightening rather than structural fixes.

2. **Recurring pattern: restating the conclusion before and after making the argument.** Several chapters introduce a point, develop it, then restate it in nearly the same words. The after-the-fact restatements were the most common cuts.

3. **"This is not X; it is Y" constructions appear frequently.** These are effective rhetorical devices but overused across the 18 chapters. No individual instance was cut for this reason alone, but a future pass could thin them.

4. **Paragraph length was generally well-controlled.** Only a handful of paragraphs exceeded 200 words. Most sections used short, focused paragraphs effectively.

5. **Hedging was minimal.** These chapters are unusually direct for academic-adjacent writing. The few instances of excess hedging ("it seems possible that perhaps") were rare and mostly appeared as qualifiers on empirical claims, where caution is appropriate.

6. **Jargon was well-managed.** Technical terms (WYSIATI, Pigovian, Pareto, Moloch trap) were consistently explained on first use and used with payoff thereafter.
