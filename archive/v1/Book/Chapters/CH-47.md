## Why a Map Chapter?

The journey is nearly complete. We began with a single axiom — that the suffering of a conscious being matters — and followed it through forty-five chapters. We built foundations, diagnosed the enemy, designed a decision architecture, lived inside it, scaled it to politics and economics, stretched it across the frontier, and tested it against every major rival tradition. Along the way, we absorbed insights from utilitarianism, Kant, Aristotle, Rawls, Marx, Confucius, the Buddha, and the traditions of care and Ubuntu. Some of our starting positions changed. Some strengthened. The system that emerged is richer and more honest than the one we began with.

A book of forty-five chapters is a long argument. Any reader who has come this far has built up a working model of the framework, but that model is distributed across hundreds of pages — scattered definitions, chapter-specific elaborations, cross-references, positions in the Registry. This chapter consolidates the whole system into one view.

The goal is not to replace what came before. The preceding chapters contain the arguments; this chapter contains the structure. A reader who has internalized the arguments can use this map as a reference. A reader who has not should not rely on the map alone -- it is a summary, not a substitute.

The map is organized around the system's architecture, which has four layers: **foundations** (what we start from), **diagnosis** (what the problem is), **architecture** (how we decide), and **applications** (where it lives).

## Layer 1: The Foundations (Book I)

### The Starting Axiom

> **The suffering of a conscious being matters.**

This is not derived. It is chosen, and defended as the minimal and hardest-to-deny normative axiom available. All other moral content in the framework derives from this plus empirical facts plus the architecture described below.

### The Grounding Claims (POS-M01 through POS-E02)

- **POS-M01: Constructivist Realism.** Moral values are constructed by conscious agents but constrained by objective features of reality (valence, game theory, evolutionary biology, cognitive limits).
- **POS-M02: Valence Realism.** Suffering and flourishing are physical features of conscious systems, not projected preferences.
- **POS-M03: Separation of Origins.** The evolutionary origin of a moral intuition does not settle its validity. Instincts must be evaluated against the axiom and the tests.
- **POS-E01: Epistemic Duty.** Truth-seeking is a moral obligation, because false beliefs produce unnecessary suffering.
- **POS-E02: Bayesian Updating.** Beliefs should be probability distributions updated on evidence.

### The Complex Value Vector

Value is not a scalar. The framework identifies six irreducible dimensions of flourishing:

1. **Hedonic tone** — the pleasure/suffering continuum
2. **Autonomy** — the capacity to direct one's own life
3. **Competence** — the exercise of developed capacities
4. **Connection** — meaningful relationships with others
5. **Understanding** — knowledge of self and world
6. **Meaning** — integration into something larger than the moment

A policy, a practice, or a way of life is evaluated by its effect on the full vector, not on any single dimension in isolation.

### The Hard Problems (CH-05)

Three things the system does not fully solve but names clearly:

- **Consciousness.** We ground value on conscious experience without having a verified theory of what consciousness is. We operate under asymmetric-cost reasoning: when uncertain whether a system is conscious, err toward inclusion.
- **Free will.** Libertarian free will is probably incompatible with physics, but compatibilism preserves enough of moral practice to proceed. This affects judgment more than it affects evaluation.
- **Moral blindness.** Every past moral framework has been blind to something its successors could see. We are almost certainly blind to analogous things now. The Position Registry architecture exists to make such blindnesses recoverable over time.

## Layer 2: The Diagnosis (Book II)

### Moloch

Most unnecessary suffering is not caused by bad people. It is caused by coordination failures -- situations where individually rational choices produce collectively catastrophic outcomes. The formal structures are well-known: Prisoner's Dilemma, Tragedy of the Commons, Stag Hunt, arms races, races to the bottom. Morality is the technology we invent to defeat these dynamics.

### The Physics of Trust

Cooperation can emerge without central authority under specific conditions. Axelrod's iterated prisoner's dilemma shows that simple reciprocal strategies (TIT FOR TAT and its variants) beat more complex exploitative strategies across a wide range of environments. The four properties of successful strategies — nice, retaliatory, forgiving, clear — are mathematically necessary features, not arbitrary virtues. The "shadow of the future" determines when cooperation is stable. Integrity is a costly signal; the cost is the feature.

### Mechanism Design

Defeating Moloch is an engineering discipline. Neither pure markets nor pure state action is universal; Ostrom demonstrated that community self-governance is a real third option with eight design principles. Pigovian corrections, cap-and-trade, commitment devices, prediction markets, nested governance -- different tools for different coordination problems. The skill is matching the tool to the problem.

### POS-A02: Morality as Coordination Technology

The unifying diagnostic claim: the function of morality is to shift agents out of bad Nash equilibria into better ones. This is not a reduction (morality is more than this in lived experience) but it is a load-bearing analysis of what morality does.

## Layer 3: The Architecture (Book III)

### Dual-Process Ethics

The central structural claim (POS-A01):

- **Mode A (default):** Follow pre-committed rules and cultivated virtues. Bias-resistant, cognitively cheap, predictability-producing, wisdom-encoding, categorically defensible. This is how most decisions should be made, most of the time.
- **Mode B (override):** Switch to explicit consequentialist calculation only when **all five** conditions are met:
  1. Catastrophic irreversible harm from rule-following
  2. Harm directly attributable to the rule
  3. Last resort — alternatives exhausted
  4. Articulable to a skeptical third party
  5. Self-interest absent or corrected

When in doubt, the rule wins. Asymmetric caution favors the default.

### The Chesterton Fence

Do not destroy inherited moral institutions you do not understand. They may encode wisdom you do not have. This is not traditionalism — inherited rules can be revised — but revision requires understanding why the rule exists before removing it.

### The Twelve Virtues (CH-11)

Virtues are firmware: installed patterns of response that the dual-process architecture runs on. Eight epistemic virtues (curiosity, relinquishment, lightness, evenness, argument/steel-manning, empiricism, simplicity, humility) and four instrumental virtues (perfectionism/tsuyoku naritai, precision, scholarship, the void). Humility is the keystone; without it every other virtue becomes a weapon.

### Moral Development

The trajectory is instinct → reflection → habit. Stage 3 is not regression to Stage 1; it is informed automaticity, where reflection has been completed and its conclusions have become second nature. Practice, community, role models, failure, and narrative are essential. Moral development is lifelong.

## Layer 4: The Applications

### Book IV — Personal Ethics

The system applied to the individual life. The daily algorithm (Chapter 13). Relationships as iterated games (Chapter 14). Meaning without God (Chapter 15). Death and the Stoic rationalist (Chapter 16).

### Book V — Political Philosophy

Eight chapters. Key positions:

- **POS-P01: Error-Correction Primacy.** Optimize for feedback loops, not outcomes.
- **POS-P02: Mechanism Design over Ideology.** Match tools to problems.
- **POS-P03: Strong Free Speech Default.** Narrow exceptions, high bar.
- **POS-P04: Constrained Democracy.** Democracy bounded by rights and federalism.
- **POS-P05: Subsidiarity.** Decisions at the lowest competent level.
- **POS-P06: Justice as Restitution + Deterrence.** Restitution, deterrence, incapacitation — retribution as vestigial.
- **POS-P07: Rights as Coordination Equilibria.** Rights are strong defaults functioning as Schelling points, with Mode A–level protection.

### Book VI — Economic Philosophy

Six chapters. Key positions:

- **POS-EC01: Markets as Information Systems.** Hayekian foundations.
- **POS-EC02: Pigovian Correction.** Align private incentives with social costs.
- **POS-EC03: Safety Net — Insurance + Capability Floor.** Insurance against uncertain risks plus capability guarantees against predictable disadvantage.
- **POS-EC04: Prediction Markets.** Epistemic infrastructure.
- **POS-EC05: Fiscal Discipline with Social Investment.** Investment-level spending debt-financeable; consumption-level spending current-financed.

### The Unified Policy Method (Chapter 24)

The same seven steps apply to political and economic questions:

1. Identify the actual problem
2. Check empirical premises
3. Analyze incentive structure (including Hayekian knowledge test and Pigovian fit test)
4. Consider alternatives
5. Estimate costs (including distributional analysis)
6. Evaluate against principles (including symmetric market-failure / government-failure analysis)
7. Decide with humility (state what would change your mind)

### Book VII — Frontier

Problems at the edge: existential risk, AI alignment, bioethics, environment, digital life. Key positions:

- **POS-F01: Existential Risk Priority.** Subject to Mode A/B constraints.
- **POS-F02: Cautious AI Development.** Coordination problem requiring mechanism design.
- **POS-F03: Consciousness-Based Moral Circle.** Asymmetric caution under uncertainty.

## The Position Registry — The Self-Correction Engine

The system is not a static set of claims. Every substantive position is registered in `Positions/POSITION_REGISTRY.md` with:

- A **confidence rating**: AXIOM, STRONG, MODERATE, PROVISIONAL, EXPLORATORY
- A **vulnerability rating**: LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL
- The chapter(s) that argue for it
- The best known objection
- The current rebuttal

When a better position is found, the old one is moved to the Archive with a note, the new one is installed, the Decisions log records the swap, and the affected chapters are revised. This is what allows the system to grow beyond its starting point. **The Framework (v12.0) was the seed; what you have read is the tree.**

## The Map in One Diagram

Imagine the system as a building:

- **The foundation** is the valence axiom plus the complex value vector. Everything rests here.
- **The frame** is the coordination analysis of Book II. It tells you what most moral problems actually are and how they work.
- **The load-bearing walls** are the dual-process architecture. They hold up the livable structure between the foundation and the roof.
- **The rooms** are the applied chapters — personal ethics, political philosophy, economic philosophy, frontier chapters. You live in these.
- **The external walls** are Book VIII (Defense). They keep the building standing against external pressure.
- **The electrical and plumbing** are the virtues — invisible when working, essential to daily life.
- **The inspection and renovation process** is the Position Registry. The building is never finished.

It is a real building. It is lived in. It is open to renovation. It is built to withstand weather.

## How the Layers Connect

The system is not modular. Each layer depends on the others:

- The **architecture** depends on the **diagnosis**: dual-process ethics is justified by the game-theoretic analysis of why rules are needed (Chapter 7).
- The **diagnosis** depends on the **foundation**: coordination analysis matters because suffering is bad, and the bad equilibria produce unnecessary suffering (Chapter 3).
- The **applications** depend on the **architecture**: specific positions in political and economic philosophy are shaped by the dual-process approach (rights as Mode A defaults, Mode B override conditions for crisis).
- The **foundation** depends, in a softer sense, on the **applications** working: if the system produced manifestly bad results in real cases, this would be evidence against the grounding. Reflective equilibrium runs in both directions.

This interdependence is a strength. It means that evidence anywhere in the system propagates to everywhere else. It is also a risk: a breakage at the foundation propagates, too. The Position Registry is the architecture for managing this risk.

## What the Map Does Not Show

Three things are not on the map, and their absence is significant:

1. **Lived experience.** The map is an abstraction. The actual framework is lived through specific choices, specific relationships, specific moments. A reader who only sees the map does not yet have the framework.

2. **The unfinished frontier.** The Expansion Zones table in `BOOK_INDEX.md` lists areas where the system is thin or silent: philosophy of mind, disability and neurodivergence, postcolonial critique, platform economy, collective action beyond the state, aesthetics, intergenerational justice beyond existential risk, deeper animal ethics. These are not failures; they are where the tree is still growing.

3. **The disagreements not yet surfaced.** The system engages many rivals but not all. Future readers will find objections we have not anticipated. When they do, the Position Registry is waiting.

## A Reader's Use of the Map

How should a reader actually use this chapter?

**As a reference.** When you can't remember which chapter argues for a specific position, this map points you there. When you want to see how two positions relate, this map shows the connection. When you want to check whether the system has a view on a given question, this map tells you yes or no (and if yes, where).

**As a stress test.** When you are evaluating a new policy, a new argument, a new claim that comes at the system from outside, use the map to check: which foundations does this depend on? Which positions does it contradict? If it succeeds, which positions would need to be swapped? The map makes the system's commitments legible so they can be challenged.

**As a check against drift.** Living with the framework over time, you will find some parts of it salient and others forgotten. The map helps you see what you are missing. If you have been focused on policy applications and neglected the foundations, the map shows you the neglect. If you have been focused on the virtues and neglected the diagnosis, likewise.

**As a guide for teaching.** If you want to explain the framework to someone else, the map is the scaffolding. Start with the axiom; build the foundations; show the diagnosis; introduce the architecture; then apply it to specific questions. The building does not need to be constructed in exactly this order every time, but the dependencies mean some things make sense only after other things are in place.

## Summary

1. **The system has four architectural layers**: foundations (axiom, grounding, value vector, hard problems), diagnosis (Moloch, trust, mechanism design), architecture (dual-process, virtues, development), and applications (personal, political, economic, frontier, defense, synthesis).

2. **The foundations** are the valence axiom plus a complex value vector, honestly acknowledging what we do not know (consciousness, free will, moral blindness).

3. **The diagnosis** is game-theoretic: most unnecessary suffering is coordination failure, and morality is the technology to defeat it.

4. **The architecture** is dual-process: rules and virtues by default, consequentialist override only when all five conditions are met, with practice and community as the developmental context.

5. **The applications** are Books IV–VII: personal ethics, political philosophy, economic philosophy, and frontier questions, with Chapter 24's unified policy method as the hinge.

6. **The defense** (Book VIII) engages rivals seriously and learns from them. The synthesis (Chapter 46) catalogs what we take.

7. **The Position Registry** is the self-correction engine. The system is designed to grow beyond its current state.

8. **The map is not the territory.** This chapter is a reference, not a substitute for the arguments. The arguments are the book.

The next chapter is the reading syllabus: a path by which a serious reader can acquire the background knowledge the framework assumes, in roughly the order the system develops them. The final chapter is the commitment — what it means to take this framework as one's own.
