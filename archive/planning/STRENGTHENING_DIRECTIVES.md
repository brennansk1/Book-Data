## Global Move 1: Claim the Unique Contribution

The audits found the book converges with sophisticated versions of every rival — leaving readers to ask what the system itself adds. The answer must be stated explicitly, in CH-01 and CH-47 at full strength and echoed in every Book VIII close:

**The framework's novel contribution is not any single position but its architecture: (1) a dual-process moral engine with a *procedural, gated* override protocol — rules by default, calculation only through bright-line gates with mandatory review; and (2) an explicit, versioned, falsifiable Position Registry — ethics run as an error-correcting engineering discipline rather than a fixed doctrine. No rival tradition ships with either. Utilitarianism has no principled brake; deontology has no principled override; virtue ethics has no decision procedure; religious ethics has no error-correction. This system has all four.**

Every Book VIII chapter ends with a short "what the framework uniquely provides against THIS rival" passage (in prose, not under a boilerplate header).

## Global Move 2: Close the Confidence Gap

Chapters must never outrun the Registry. Where a chapter defends a PROVISIONAL or HIGH-vulnerability position (rights CH-19, justice/desert CH-22, fiscal CH-31, consciousness scope CH-05/CH-35, free speech CH-21), the chapter names the weakness in its own voice. CH-22 is the model: "This is unsatisfying. It is also the honest conclusion."

## Global Move 3: Fight the Named Opponent

Name-dropping without engagement is the audits' most repeated finding. Each defense chapter must engage the *specific argument* of the one most dangerous contemporary defender (assignments below). One deep engagement beats five names.

## Global Move 4: The Companions-in-Guilt Argument (new weapon)

Deploy in CH-03 and CH-38: the argument from epistemic normativity. Mackie's queerness argument says objective values would be metaphysically weird. But *epistemic* norms — "you ought to believe what your evidence supports" — are normative in exactly the same way, and no one can reject them without self-refutation, since rejecting them requires citing evidence. Anyone who argues at all is committed to irreducible normativity. Moral normativity is therefore not *uniquely* queer; the anti-realist's own practice smuggles in the same kind of fact. This shifts the burden: the error theorist must explain why epistemic oughts are acceptable while moral oughts are not, when both are grounded the same way in the framework (both derive from what conscious agents require to navigate reality). This is a recognized, powerful move (Cuneo's *The Normative Web*; cite by name, paraphrase not quote).

## Global Move 5: Precautionary Patienthood (new doctrine)

The moral-circle detection problem (POS-F03) is the system's most exposed flank. Install a single consistent doctrine in CH-05, and apply it in CH-33 (AI) and CH-35 (animals/environment):

**Expected-valence moral weight.** We cannot detect consciousness directly; we can estimate its probability from convergent markers (nociceptive architecture, integrative processing, flexible tradeoff behavior, self-modeling). Moral weight = probability of sentience × capacity for valence. Under uncertainty, run precaution proportional to stakes and cheapness of caution (Birch's precautionary framework for sentience; paraphrase with attribution). This converts an undetectable-property objection into a manageable decision-under-uncertainty problem — the same move insurance and existential-risk reasoning already make elsewhere in the book. Name the residual honestly: probability assignments for radically non-mammalian architectures (insects, AI) remain rough; the doctrine disciplines the uncertainty, it does not eliminate it.
