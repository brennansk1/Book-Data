### Nice-to-Have — Would Strengthen Specific Chapters

| # | Title | Author | Priority | Needed For | Why |
|---|-------|--------|----------|------------|-----|
| MR-011 | A Theory of Justice | John Rawls | HIGH | CH-41, CH-19, CH-28 | The single most important work in 20th-century political philosophy. Our system's insurance argument is close to the veil of ignorance — we must engage Rawls in full depth, not just summarize him. Critical for the Contractualism debate chapter. |
| MR-012 | Anarchy, State, and Utopia | Robert Nozick | MEDIUM | CH-19, CH-25, CH-41 | The strongest libertarian rights theory. Our rights-as-coordination-equilibria position (POS-P07) must survive Nozick's side-constraint argument. Also essential contrast to Rawls. |
| MR-013 | Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong | J.L. Mackie | MEDIUM | CH-01, CH-03, CH-37 | Error theory is the most sophisticated version of moral anti-realism. Our valence realism must beat Mackie's argument that moral properties are "queer." Key for the Nihilism debate chapter. |
| MR-014 | Taking Rights Seriously | Ronald Dworkin | LOW | CH-19, OQ-005 | Rights as trumps — the leading alternative to our coordination-equilibria account. Would sharpen CH-19 significantly. |
