### CH-01: The Third Way — Why We Need a New Moral System

Core argument: the two historical answers to what grounds morality have both failed. The Theistic Map grounds right and wrong in an authority whose foundations cannot be checked, so it cannot correct its own worst impulses. The Nihilistic Map (Hume's is-ought gap, Darwinian debunking, Nietzsche's death of God) correctly diagnoses that failure but supplies no replacement, and the vacuum gets filled by whatever authority is nearest. Constructivist Realism is offered as the third option: values are constructed by conscious agents but constrained by three objective features of reality — valence, game theory/Moloch, and evolutionary psychology. Seven specifications a viable system must meet are laid out; no rival meets all seven, and the chapter lands its payoff: the system's real originality is architectural (a gated dual-process procedure plus a versioned, falsifiable Position Registry), not any borrowed axiom. The one chosen axiom — "the suffering of a conscious being matters" — is defended via performative incoherence, explicitly conceded as pragmatic, not proof.

Opening case: the Nuremberg trials (Jackson's opening statement, Keitel's "superior orders" defense), returned to at the close. Integrated cases: the Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse (1940); Milgram's 1961 obedience experiments. Quotes: Jackson, Hume (*Treatise* III.1.1), Nietzsche (*Gay Science* §125), all canonical.
