### CH-04: The Alien God — Evolution, Instinct, and Reflective Rebellion
**Status:** REVISED (Second Edition) | **Date:** 2026-07-12 | **Revision:** 2

**Core Argument:** Opens with Darwin's own confession that his theory felt "like confessing a murder," and his extension of natural selection to the moral sense in *The Descent of Man* — establishing that our instincts were built by an indifferent optimizer (the Alien God), not a benevolent designer. Haidt's six Moral Foundations (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity, Liberty) form humanity's evolved "first draft." The genetic fallacy cuts both ways: origin never settles validity. Three tests — valence, coordination, epistemic accuracy — sort instincts into keep (empathy, fairness, the hive switch) and discard-and-replace (tribalism, blind deference, purity-based dehumanization). The featured objection is now Sharon Street's evolutionary debunking argument, pressed in its strongest form as an infinite regress of intuitions judging intuitions; the chapter answers that the regress terminates at exactly two chosen, non-derived points — the valence axiom (CH-03) and epistemic duty (CH-02) — because denying either is self-defeating for a suffering, reasoning being. The Honest Ledger names the falsification condition explicitly rather than merely asserting confidence. Closes on the dual nature (chimp and bee) and hands off to Chapter 9's rule architecture as the engineering response.
