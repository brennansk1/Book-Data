## Critical Unresolved Questions

### OQ-001: The Is-Ought Gap (Hume's Guillotine)
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** CRITICAL | **Needed For:** CH-01, CH-03
**Problem:** The system claims to derive moral "oughts" from empirical facts. But Hume argued you cannot derive an "ought" from an "is." This is the single most dangerous objection to the entire project.
**Current Thinking:** The system doesn't derive ought from is — it starts with a minimal axiom (conscious suffering matters) and uses empirical facts to determine optimal strategies. The axiom is the bridge. But this needs rigorous, bulletproof treatment.
**What Would Settle It:** Engagement with Hume, Moore's naturalistic fallacy, and modern responses (e.g., Railton's moral realism, Searle's institutional facts, Mackie's error theory). May need user to provide key texts.

### OQ-002: The Scope of Valence and Moral Patienthood
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH | **Needed For:** CH-03, CH-05, CH-34
**Problem:** If valence is the foundation, what about animals, ecosystems, future AIs, potential people? Where is the line and why?
**Current Thinking:** Consciousness is necessary for moral patienthood. But we have no reliable test for consciousness. Need practical heuristics under uncertainty.
**What Would Settle It:** Engagement with Singer (animal ethics), Chalmers (hard problem), Tononi (IIT), and the moral uncertainty literature.

### OQ-003: The Mode A/B Threshold
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH | **Needed For:** CH-09, CH-10
**Problem:** When exactly does one switch from rule-following to consequentialist calculation? The "1% of life" framing is vague and gameable.
**Current Thinking:** Threshold requires: (a) irreversible catastrophic harm from following the rule, (b) articulable justification to a skeptical third party, (c) no self-serving interest in the override.
**What Would Settle It:** Need to formalize this with concrete examples and stress-test it against historical cases (e.g., would this framework have produced the right answer in the Jim Crow South? In wartime? In a pandemic?).

### OQ-004: Justice and Desert
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH | **Needed For:** CH-22
**Problem:** The framework's justice position (POS-P06) drops retribution entirely. But many people's deepest moral intuitions include a sense of "desert" — that wrongdoers *deserve* punishment independent of its consequences.
**Current Thinking:** Need to either (a) defend dropping desert and explain why the intuition is misleading, or (b) reconstruct desert within the framework (perhaps: desert as a Schelling point that enables trust?).
**What Would Settle It:** Engagement with retributivism (Kant, Moore), expressivism (Feinberg, Duff), and the psychological literature on desert intuitions.

### OQ-005: Rights as Coordination Equilibria vs. Rights as Trumps
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH | **Needed For:** CH-19
**Problem:** If rights are just coordination equilibria (POS-P07), they can be overridden when the equilibrium shifts. But the whole point of rights is that they CAN'T be overridden by majority will. This is a tension in the system.
**Current Thinking:** Possibly resolve by making rights Mode A defaults with an extremely high bar for Mode B override. But this may not satisfy a Dworkinian who thinks rights are genuinely lexically prior.
**What Would Settle It:** Deep engagement with Dworkin (rights as trumps), Nozick (side constraints), and Hohfeld (rights analysis).

### OQ-006: The Safety Net — Beyond Insurance
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** MEDIUM | **Needed For:** CH-28
**Problem:** The insurance framing for welfare (POS-EC03) doesn't handle predictable disadvantage (born into poverty, disability, systemic discrimination). Insurance covers uncertain risks, not known inequalities.
**Current Thinking:** May need to expand to include (a) capability-based arguments (Sen, Nussbaum), (b) a "floor of dignity" concept, or (c) fairness arguments that go beyond pure insurance.
**What Would Settle It:** Engagement with Sen (capabilities approach), Nussbaum, Rawls (difference principle), and the basic income literature.

### OQ-007: Non-Western Moral Traditions
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** MEDIUM | **Needed For:** CH-45, Expansion Zones
**Problem:** The framework draws almost exclusively from Western thinkers. If the system claims to be the "best possible moral system," it must engage with and potentially absorb insights from Confucianism, Buddhism, Ubuntu philosophy, Islamic ethics, Hindu dharmic traditions, etc.
**Current Thinking:** Not just debate opponents — genuine sources of insight. Confucian role ethics, Buddhist compassion and non-attachment, Ubuntu's communal ontology may fill real gaps in the system.
**What Would Settle It:** User may need to provide texts. Key needs: Mencius, Analects, Dhammapada, basic Ubuntu philosophy texts.

### OQ-008: The Free Speech Paradox
**Raised:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** MEDIUM | **Needed For:** CH-21
**Problem:** The system's own game theory suggests that unregulated information environments can be exploited (disinformation as a coordination attack). The "marketplace of ideas" may itself be subject to Moloch dynamics.
**Current Thinking:** Need to distinguish between speech-as-expression (protect) and speech-as-coordination-attack (different category). But this distinction is difficult to draw without creating censorship tools that will be abused.
**What Would Settle It:** Engagement with Mill (harm principle), Schelling (focal points in information environments), and modern work on epistemic security.

### MR-005: "Governing the Commons" — Elinor Ostrom
**Requested:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH
**Needed For:** CH-08 (Mechanism Design), CH-27 (Externalities & Public Goods)
**Why:** This is a **critical blind spot** in the framework. The framework treats coordination failures as solvable by either markets or government. Ostrom won the Nobel Prize for showing a third option: community-managed commons with specific design principles. Her 8 institutional design principles for sustainable common-pool resource management are directly relevant to the system's core thesis (morality as coordination technology). Without Ostrom, our mechanism design chapter is incomplete and vulnerable to the objection "you only offer markets or state."
**What I'm Looking For:** The 8 design principles, the specific case studies (irrigation systems, fisheries, grazing commons), and the conditions under which community governance succeeds vs. fails.
**Status:** REQUESTED

### MR-006: "After Virtue" — Alasdair MacIntyre
**Requested:** 2026-03-26 | **Priority:** HIGH
**Needed For:** CH-42 (Against Virtue Ethics), CH-01 (The Third Way), CH-11 (Virtues)
**Why:** MacIntyre's critique is the **most dangerous attack** on the entire Enlightenment moral project — which is essentially what our system is. He argues that modern moral philosophy (including exactly the kind of constructivist project we're building) is incoherent because it ripped moral concepts out of the Aristotelian teleological framework that gave them meaning. If we can't answer MacIntyre, our system has a foundational crack. I know his general thesis but not the detailed argument well enough to either refute or absorb it properly.
**What I'm Looking For:** The "disquieting suggestion" argument, the emotivism diagnosis, the narrative account of virtue, the argument that modern moral philosophy lacks a shared telos, and his positive Aristotelian alternative.
**Status:** REQUESTED
